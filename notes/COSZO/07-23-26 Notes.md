### COSZO Operations meeting


**Meeting Objective & Timeline** The primary focus of the meeting was to transition from the installation phase to the operations phase and determine the associated costs and logistical responsibilities. The team anticipates moving into a pre-operation stage once the data streams are established and metadata is complete, with the **commissioning phase targeted for the summer of 2027**.

**Data Management and QA/QC Strategy**

- **Data Streaming:** The system will generate a massive amount of data (approximately 450 channels) that will stream into EarthScope. The data will not be made public until the associated metadata is completed.
- **Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC):** The current funding does not cover dedicated, manual QA/QC processes. Instead, the team plans to leverage a hybrid approach:
    - **Artificial Intelligence:** The team plans to use AI to efficiently monitor the data, specifically looking to apply for an NSF call regarding OOI (Ocean Observatories Initiative) and AI. Potential tools include generating daily notifications for timing issues and publishing hourly spectrograms to easily spot "wonky" data.
    - **Crowdsourcing:** Data will be released to the community with a disclaimer that it is in a "commissioning phase". By building an active user base, the team expects the community to naturally flag data anomalies or outages, much like how users currently report earthquake catalog crashes.
- **Handling Data Outages:** Because EarthScope/IRIS does not have a system for annotating large network outages or missing data periods, the team plans to document known issues on their own website. The EarthScope metadata for every channel will include a link pointing users back to the COSZO website to view these known issues.

**System Integration: RCA vs. Independent PI Experiment** A major point of discussion was whether the COSZO network should be fully integrated into the existing **Regional Cabled Array (RCA)** or remain an independent Principal Investigator (PI) experiment.

- **Reporting Requirements:** Integrating with the RCA system would mean adopting their stringent reporting requirements, which currently involve weekly, monthly, quarterly, and annual reports to the NSF. The COSZO team strongly prefers checking data on a monthly time scale rather than weekly.
- **Hidden Costs of Integration:** If the COSZO network is integrated into the RCA, the budget must accurately reflect the additional administrative and management burdens. Currently, the cost estimates cover engineering functions (like APL turning around a junction box every 3 to 5 years), but they fail to account for the time spent on permitting, Navy interactions, managing PI expansion ports, and submitting reports.

**Concept of Operations (ConOps) and Budgeting**

- The current ConOps plan lacks the necessary detail regarding long-term administrative costs and community management.
- **Expansion Ports:** The team noted that the COSZO junction box features expansion ports specifically funded for subduction zone monitoring. Any future proposals to add instruments to these ports (especially non-subduction related requests) will require clear guidance and approval from the NSF.

**Community Engagement**

- Maintaining an active user community is vital for identifying data issues and justifying the system's value.
- The operations plan includes funding for an **early career workshop**, which will likely be scheduled about six months after the data is confirmed to be of good quality.
- The team also discussed integrating students into upcoming cruises, allowing them to sail and gain hands-on experience.
