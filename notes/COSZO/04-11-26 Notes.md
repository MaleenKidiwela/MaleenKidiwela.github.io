## Using sp_calc, sp_fit, sp_nominal to characterize day-to-day timing

### What each metric measures

| Metric | Definition | What it tells you |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| `sp_nominal` | Fixed from param file (e.g. 1.000000 s) | What the sensor *should* do — never changes |
| `sp_calc` | Robust median of inter-sample intervals (lower 90th percentile Δt) | What the sensor *typically* does that day — resistant to gaps and outliers |
| `sp_fit` | Least-squares slope through all timestamps | Average rate across the full day — sensitive to start/end position of data |

### Why they can differ

- **sp_calc ≠ sp_nominal** → clock running fast or slow relative to design spec
- **sp_fit ≠ sp_calc** → interval distribution is skewed; some very short or long intervals pull the mean away from the median. Strong skewness in residuals (Panel 1) is a symptom.
- **sp_fit ≠ sp_nominal** → overall day-level drift from nominal when integrated across the whole day

### Idea: use these three to track inter-day differences

Plot `sp_calc − sp_nominal`, `sp_fit − sp_nominal`, and `sp_calc − sp_fit` as time series across all days in the metrics CSV. This would reveal:

1. **Long-term clock drift** — is sp_calc consistently above/below nominal over weeks/months? Would show up as a persistent offset in `sp_calc − sp_nominal`.
2. **Day-to-day variability** — scatter in `sp_calc` from day to day may indicate instrument instability or environmental effects (pressure, temperature).
3. **Skewness signature** — large `sp_calc − sp_fit` on a given day means the interval distribution is asymmetric. Combined with residual skewness metric, this flags days with unusual timing behaviour that isn't just clock drift.
4. **Deployment transitions** — expect a step change in sp_calc at deployment boundaries (dep1 = 15 s, dep2 = 1 s). The series should be flat within each deployment.

### Example from 2026-01-07 (RS01SLBS dep2)

- sp_nominal = 1.000000 s
- sp_calc    = 1.000502 s  (+0.502 ms above nominal — clock slightly slow)
- sp_fit     = 1.000000 s  (+0.000 ms — mean rate equals nominal for this day)
- sp_calc − sp_fit = +0.502 ms — skewed distribution; median > mean

The discrepancy between sp_calc and sp_fit is the skewness signature: most intervals are 1.000502 s but enough short intervals exist to bring the day-average back to 1.000000 s. Panel 1 residuals show skewness = −3.54, confirming this.

### Suggested summary figure addition

In `plot_mode` Figure 1 (sample rate over time), consider adding a third scatter series for `sp_fit` in addition to `sp_calc`, with the three reference lines. Over a long time series, divergence between sp_calc and sp_fit trends would indicate changing skewness character in the data.

### Key clarifications

- Panel 1 vs Panel 2: best-fit removes slope AND intercept (residuals centred at 0); nominal removes only slope (intercept fixed at origin). Even when sp_fit ≈ sp_nominal, Panel 2 is shifted by the intercept b_fit ≈ mean(t_sec) − sp_fit × mean(idx).
- Panel 4 ramp slope = sp_calc − sp_nominal (clock drift rate from nominal).
- Panel 5 replaces the old Δt scatter — vertical bands are easier to read for gap location and duration.
