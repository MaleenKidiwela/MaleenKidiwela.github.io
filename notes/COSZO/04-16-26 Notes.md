## Figure 4 — Sample count per day

Rebuilt the meaning of "expected" twice.

**Problem:** the old `expected_npts` is derived from the observed `data_end − data_start` window, so a day with only 6 hours of data self-validates as "full".

**Fix 1 — nominal full-day reference:** bars now coloured by comparison to `86400 / sp_nominal`:
- green — `n_points ≥ 86400/sp_nominal − 1` (full calendar day)
- red — below that (samples missing)
- grey — no data
- dashed purple line at the nominal full-day count

**Fix 2 — per-day fit overlay:** added an amber line at `86400 / sp_fit[day]` — the number of samples a full calendar day would hold at that day's *measured* rate. Any bar falling below its own amber line = missing samples even relative to the day's fitted (drifted) rate.

## Q&A captured during session

- **`n_points`** = number of samples actually in that day's file after loading/gap-splitting (`len(t_sec)`). Nothing more.
- **Gap splitting** = within a day's timestamp array, flag intervals `dt > multiplier × sp_calc` as gaps (`diagnose_timing.py:448–470`), then `np.split(t_sec, gap_idx+1)` partitions samples into contiguous segments. No samples are dropped — just grouped into runs separated by gaps. Used downstream for per-segment stats (`sp_seg`, jitter).
- **`expected_npts`** = `round((data_end − data_start) / sp_calc) + 1`. Based on the *observed* data window, not a calendar day. Flagged as misleading for full-day coverage → that's why Figure 4 now uses `86400 / sp_nominal` and `86400 / sp_fit[day]` instead.
