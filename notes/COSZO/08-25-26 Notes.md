### Update on the COSZO cruise

The cruise has been delayed because the bow thruster of the Roger Revelle is leaking oil and needs to be repaired before it can be used for anything other navigational emergencies.  This requires the ship enter a dry dock. The Marine Superintendent at the Scripps Institution of Oceanography (SIO) states that this will take 10 days in the dry dock.  SIO are still searching for available dry docks up and down the West Coast. As of yesterday they had not located a dry dock that is immediately available, so there will very likely be some delay beyond transit time, before the dry dock can start.

Both William and Bill  who is leading the Jason cruise following COSZO, have submitted ideas for a shortened cruise that could get most of our objectives accomplished.  For COSZO this idea, involves 7 days on site (3 to deploy all our infrastructure except the seismometers, 1 to test the seismometer installations and perhaps install 1, 1 day for the SIO strain meter and 2 days for a little transit between sites and contingency).

There is a weather window for Jason work off the coast with the chances of bad weather getting progressively higher after mid-September with a cuffoff that is typically the start of October or mid-October in a pinch. 

At this point it is unclear if the Revelle will have time for 0, 1 or both the shortened Jason cruises.

At present, all the COSZO and SIO gear is loaded and tied down with the idea that if a plan emerges to sail the COSZO cruise after the dry dock, then the equipment might be able to stay on board (depending on the shipyard) with the cruise departing directly from the shipyard.  If that is not the final plan, then the equipment can be offloaded at short notice.
