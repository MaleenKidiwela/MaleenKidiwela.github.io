
## Subsurface Beach Monitoring Using Fiber Optics and Ocean Waves

## Presentation Overview & Research Team

- **Speaker:** Hyponyi (Stanford University).
- **Collaborators & Institutions:** Hannah Glober and Mig Wingu (Oregon State University), Fu (Rice University), and Gingsha (Nanyang Technological University).
- **Core Research Question:** Can ambient seismic energy generated by breaking ocean waves be leveraged via fiber-optic sensing to image and monitor dynamic subsurface processes beneath sandy beaches?
- **Field Demonstration Site:** A coastal beach setup inspired by observations across the Pacific in Punga Bay, where wave run-up and sediment wetting provide substantial mechanical energy.

---
## 1. Motivation & Coastal Hazards

- **Vulnerability of Sandy Beaches:** Coastal sandy beaches worldwide face mounting threats from extreme storms, accelerated coastal erosion, and seawater intrusion into coastal aquifers.
- **Recent Impact Example:** A contemporary NBC Los Angeles report highlighted severe high-surf flooding that inundated beachfront communities in Newport Beach.
- **The Monitoring Imperative:** Effective coastal hazard mitigation requires continuous subsurface observation of coastal aquifers and their hydraulic properties to track groundwater dynamics and seawater movement.

---

## 2. Challenges in Transition-Zone Geophysical Imaging

- **The "Shallow Water Gap":** The transition zone between open ocean and dry land is notoriously difficult to survey using traditional geophysical tools:
    - **Offshore Limitations:** Deep-water marine vessels deploying air guns cannot operate in very shallow surf zones.
    - **Onshore Limitations:** Heavy vibroseis trucks and active seismic vibrators are physically intrusive and ecologically disruptive to pristine beach environments and local communities.
- **Emerging Frontier:** The Society of Exploration Geophysicists (SEG) recently hosted a dedicated workshop in Malaysia focusing on shallow-water and transition-zone geophysical applications for both coastal hazard mitigation and energy exploration.

---

## 3. Experimental Setup & Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS)

- **Fiber-Optic Deployment:**
    - **Cable Layout:** A single fiber-optic sensing cable spanning **180 meters** was installed perpendicular to the coastline, running from the dry beach into the submerged surf zone.
    - **Burial Details:** The fiber was trenched and buried at a depth of approximately **15 to 30 cm** directly within the beach sediment.
- **Zonational Framework of the Beach Profile:**
    - **Backshore:** The upper, dry section above the high-tide line that remains free of direct water submergence.
    - **Intertidal Zone:** The dynamic intermediate zone subject to periodic tidal wetting and wave run-up.
    - **Foreshore:** The lower beach face positioned below the low-tide line that remains continuously submerged.

---

## 4. Wave Regimes, Wave Speeds & Spectral Characteristics

- **Spatial Reference:** In the recordings, $0\text{ m}$ marks the dry shoreward end, with increasing distance extending seaward.
- **Low-Frequency Wave Run-up ($<1\text{ Hz}$):**
    - Five-minute continuous DAS recordings demonstrate that wave run-up extends far inland during high tide, whereas during low tide wave action reaches only midway along the cable.
- **Wave Velocity Disparities:**
    - **Hydrodynamic Ocean Waves:** Propagate shoreward at apparent speeds of **~5 m/s** during high tide and decelerate to **~2.5 m/s** at low tide due to shallow-water shoaling.
    - **Seismic Shear Waves:** Move through the sandy sediment at approximately **180 m/s**, a velocity characteristic of shallow unconsolidated sand.
- **Spectral Energy Regimes (Power Spectral Density - PSD):**
    - **Submerged Foreshore:** Dominated by direct hydrodynamic water loading that follows a linear trend in the log domain, governed by a power law scaling with frequency ($f^{-0.2}$).
    - **Subaerial Backshore:** Dominated by seismic wave energy in the **1 to 40 Hz** frequency band.
    - **Seismic Source Mechanism:** The 1–40 Hz seismic energy is generated by breaking surf, representing a distinct mechanism from lower-frequency planetary microseisms.
    - **Tidal Energy Modulation:** When intertidal channels are submerged at high tide, direct hydrodynamic loading overwhelms the signal; when exposed at low tide, the same channels record predominantly seismic wavefield energy.

---

## 5. Subsurface Characterization via Seismic Interferometry

- **Virtual Source Reconstruction:** Cross-correlating continuous ambient noise across DAS channels extracts coherent virtual source gathers.
- **Gather Asymmetry:** Cross-correlation gathers exhibit strong directional asymmetry because the ambient seismic wavefield is predominantly generated by incoming ocean breakers directed landward.
- **Multimodal Dispersion Curves:**
    - Dispersion curves clearly resolve both fundamental and higher-order surface wave modes across both foreshore and backshore arrays.
- **Shear-Wave Velocity ($V_s$) Inversion:**
    - Inversion routines incorporated water-layer boundary conditions to match physical interface constraints.
    - **Vadose Zone Identification:** The subaerial backshore exhibits anomalously high shear-wave velocities. This is interpreted as the **vadose (unsaturated) zone**, where capillary suction (matric potential) creates negative pore pressure, increasing effective stress and sediment shear modulus.
    - **Structural Profiling:** Inversion successfully maps the depth profile of the unconfined coastal aquifer, the depositional layering of sand, and the aquifer base.

---

## 6. Time-Lapse Monitoring & Relative Velocity Changes ($dv/v$)

- **Dynamic Travel-Time Shifts:** Time-lapse monitoring of individual trace offsets reveals subtle arrival-time shifts that directly reflect relative seismic velocity changes ($dv/v$).
- **Spatiotemporal $dv/v$ Mapping:**
    - Calculating $dv/v$ across all source-receiver pairs reveals dynamic velocity oscillations tightly coupled to the tidal cycle.
    - Blue values indicate velocity decreases, while red values indicate velocity increases.
- **Inland Phase Delay:**
    - While foreshore velocity changes oscillate in direct phase with tidal water elevation, backshore and intertidal velocity variations exhibit a distinct **time lag**.
    - The quantified delay time progressively increases with distance inland away from the shoreline.

---

## 7. Dual-Mechanism Physical Modeling Framework

To explain the disparate phase responses across the beach profile, the researchers coupled two distinct numerical modeling frameworks:

## Mechanism A: Fluid-Solid Coupled Elastic Wave Propagation (Foreshore)

- **Spectral Element Modeling:** Numerical simulations modeled a coupled fluid-solid elastic system across varying tidal stages.
- **Water-Depth Modulation:** The simulation maintained **constant sediment material properties** and varied only the overlying water depth.
- **Wave Conversion:** Surface waves propagate as **Scholte waves** beneath the submerged foreshore and transition into **Rayleigh waves** across dry sediment.
- **Apparent Velocity Variations:** Because seismic wavelengths in this shallow-water setting are comparable to the water depth, tidal fluctuations dynamically alter boundary conditions and induce apparent arrival-time delays independently of sediment changes.

## Mechanism B: Hydromechanical Groundwater Diffusion (Intertidal & Backshore)

- **Dupuit-Boussinesq Flow Formulation:** Unconfined groundwater flow was simulated using the Dupuit-Boussinesq equation driven by tidal head variations at the beach face.
- **Capillary Retention:** Incorporating moisture retention relationships above the water table accounted for capillary suction and effective stress transitions.
- **Rock Physics Coupling:** Hydromechanical groundwater simulations coupled with rock physics models successfully reproduced observed $dv/v$ curves.
- **Tidal Hysteresis:** The system exhibits clear hysteresis, where the subsurface hydromechanical response follows different trajectories during rising (flood) versus falling (ebb) tides.

## Integrated Synthesis:

- **Foreshore Dynamics:** Primarily governed by fluid-solid coupled elastic boundary effects and water-depth changes.
- **Backshore and Intertidal Dynamics:** Primarily driven by groundwater flow, pore-pressure diffusion, and effective stress modulation.

---

## 8. Key Conclusions & Practical Applications

- **Ambient Energy as a Passive Probe:** Ocean breaking waves provide a continuous, energetic, and non-destructive seismic source for interrogating the difficult-to-access coastal transition zone.
- **Aquifer Hydrogeology:** Time-lapse DAS measurements allow researchers to delineate aquifer stratification, map the aquifer base, and directly constrain hydraulic diffusivity.
- **Pore Pressure & Geotechnical Monitoring:** Relating $dv/v$ shifts to effective stress theory enables non-invasive monitoring of pore pressure within coastal aquifers.
- **Coastal Resilience:** Provides real-time observational constraints to track seawater intrusion and forecast coastal vulnerability prior to extreme storm and high-surf seasons.

## Talk 1: Multimodal Borehole Observatory at the San Andreas Fault Observatory at Depth (SAFOD)

- **Speaker & Affiliation:** Andy (USGS / Stanford University collaboration, with past support from NSF and ICDP).

## 1. Geological Setting and Historical Baseline

- **Tectonic Location:** The San Andreas Fault Observatory at Depth (**SAFOD**) is situated in central California, representing the first deep scientific borehole drilled directly into an active plate-boundary fault zone at seismogenic depths.
- **Deformation Zones:** The borehole traverses the actively creeping segment of the San Andreas Fault, directly penetrating two narrow, actively creeping shear zones: the **Southwest Deforming Zone (SDZ)** and the **Central Deforming Zone (CDZ)**. Active casing deformation across these zones has been repeatedly documented by caliper surveys.
- **Damage Zone and Guided Waves:** Structural logging reveals an overarching **~200-meter-wide low-velocity damage zone**. A downhole geophone deployed in the borehole recorded deep microearthquakes (~7 km focal depth, ~4 km beneath the sensor) exhibiting both **P- and S-type fault-zone guided waves**, confirming that the low-velocity damage zone extends continuously to seismogenic depths.
- **Fault Core Geology:** Core retrieved from the SDZ revealed foliated serpentinized fault gouge surrounding a massive serpentinite block, characterized by **ultra-low friction coefficients** that facilitate aseismic creep.
- **Targeting Repeating Earthquakes:** Drilling aimed to intersect repeating microearthquake patches designated **SF, LA, and HI** exhibiting quasi-synchronized recurrence. The borehole missed the asperities directly, terminating within **100–150 m** of the Hawaii patch and within several hundred meters of SF and LA.

## 2. Downhole Instrumentation and Recent Upgrades

- **Environmental Constraints:** Downhole monitoring is severely challenged by ambient temperatures of **115°C to 120°C**, corrosive borehole fluids, and dissolved gases. Over the project's lifetime, **30 successive geophone packages** have been deployed.
- **March 2024 Array Deployment:** A newly installed, heavily armored multimodal cable contains **three buffer tubes housing six optical fibers each**, integrated electrical conductors, and a **two-component geophone** at the deep termination.
- **Optical Interrogation Design:** The fiber is configured in a loop, permitting laser pulses to travel down the hole and return to the surface for complete two-way monitoring.
- **Surface Complement:** The subsurface fiber is integrated with a surface **broadband seismometer**, a **high-rate GNSS station**, and a **meteorological station** recording ambient weather outside and within the interrogator enclosure.
- **Interrogator Acquisition:** Distributed Acoustic Sensing (**DAS**) data are sampled continuously at **1 kHz** with a **2 m channel spacing**. Logging utilized a Silixa QuantX and OptaSense/Intella-Nano interrogator, hot-swapping units after a photodetector malfunction to preserve temporal continuity.

## 3. Low-Frequency Strain Observations and Environmental Decoupling

- **Post-Deployment Strain Signals:** Low-frequency strain recordings (**≤1 Hz**) revealed massive post-installation strain relaxation concentrated near specific cable sections.
- **Thermal and Barometric Contamination:** Raw low-frequency strain exhibited widespread, depth-independent temporal variations caused by environmental fluctuations. Thermal noise stems from solar exposure on the suspended cable section between the wellhead and the instrument shelter, as well as ambient temperature swings inside the interrogator enclosure.
- **Coherence Correction:** Coherence analysis between meteorological sensors and strain channels showed a distinct transition around **8-hour periods**. Applying an empirical transfer-function correction using internal and external weather telemetry removed these broad fluctuations, isolating authentic subsurface deformation.

## 4. Episodic Transients and High-Frequency Seismicity

- **Bottom-Hole Episodic Transients:** Environmental correction unmasked rapid-onset, **multi-hour episodic strain transients** concentrated within the bottom **100 m** of the borehole.
- **Precursory Mechanics:** These episodic transients are immediately preceded by **localized static strain** across symmetric zones of the downward and upward fiber paths, accompanied by **high-frequency seismic energy** recorded on the bottom geophone.
- **Physical Interpretations:** The working hypothesis attributes these transients to **mechanical cable slip** under residual deployment tension; however, their decay kinematics closely resemble **dynamic velocity-weakening fault slip** or a **standard linear solid (viscous) rheology**.
- **Microearthquake Captures:** Operating at 1 kHz, the DAS and geophone array successfully detected microearthquakes (**M ≤ 1.0**) originating from the SF/LA repeating cluster, displaying rich wavefield complexity, fault-zone guided waves, and strong waveform coherence with historical events (e.g., matching a 2006 event from the same patch).
- **Associated Studies:** Complementary research includes travel-time delay analyses using active/passive sources (Natalia) and 3D starting velocity models for **Full Waveform Inversion (FWI)** of fault structure (Alina).

## 5. Future Plans and Data Access

- **Drilling Proposals:** An ICDP workshop proposal is in preparation to evaluate drilling new multilateral sidetracks to directly intersect the repeating earthquake source.
- **Instrumentation Goals:** Future re-instrumentation targets include **Distributed Temperature Sensing (DTS)**, **Distributed Strain Sensing (DSS)**, **tensor strainmeters**, and downhole fluid-pressure monitoring.
- **Surface Extensions:** Extending fiber along the surface trace through the **Parkfield segment**, which has reached its historical median recurrence interval.
- **Data Release:** Low-frequency continuous datasets (~gigabytes) and event-windowed 1 kHz high-frequency waveforms will be distributed to the scientific community.

## Talk 2: The Global SMART Subsea Cables Initiative

- **Speaker & Affiliation:** Helen, presenting on behalf of Bruce Howe (University of Hawaii).

## 1. Organizational Governance and Mission

- **Background:** The SMART Subsea Cables effort has been developing since **2011**.
- **Joint Task Force (JTF):** Governed by the **JTF for SMART Cables**, a UN-sanctioned initiative encompassing **over 400 volunteer experts** across oceanography, geophysics, telecommunications engineering, law, and business.
- **Operating Model:** The JTF acts as an international coordinating body providing technical frameworks, standard functional specifications, and commercial advocacy. Individual regional projects obtain their own funding and negotiate bilateral commercial partnerships with cable owners, resulting in modular system implementations.

## 2. Core Technical Architecture: SMART vs. DAS

- **The SMART Concept:** Rather than funding dedicated, cost-prohibitive scientific subsea cables, the initiative **integrates scientific point sensors directly into the optical repeaters of commercial telecommunication cables** for a marginal cost increase.
- **Primary Sensor Suite:**
    1. **Ocean-bottom temperature sensors**.
    2. **Absolute pressure sensors** (ocean-bottom pressure recorders for tsunamis and ocean dynamic height).
    3. **Three-component seismic sensors** (broadband seismometers or accelerometers).
- **Node Spacing:** Sensors are integrated at standard optical amplifier repeaters spaced **50 to 100 km apart** along transoceanic routes.
- **Comparison with DAS:**
    - _Sensing Mode:_ SMART deploys calibrated **discrete point sensors** measuring 3D vector velocity/acceleration, absolute pressure, and temperature; DAS measures **1D axial strain rate** along fiber cores.
    - _Coverage & Span:_ SMART delivers basin-wide coverage across thousands of kilometers at 50–100 km intervals; transoceanic DAS remains an evolving frontier technology.
    - _Calibration Synergy:_ SMART seismometers possess fully characterized instrumental response functions, serving as **calibrated ground-truth anchor points** to benchmark and constrain uncalibrated subsea DAS strain measurements. Seafloor coupling of heavy repeater housings remains an active engineering focus.

| Parameter | SMART Cable Sensors | Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensor Type** | Discrete point sensors in repeaters | Continuous optical fiber core |
| **Observables** | 3C seismic, absolute pressure, temp | 1D axial strain / strain rate |
| **Spatial Resolution** | 50–100 km repeater spacing | Metric channel spacing (~1–10 m) |
| **Instrumental Calibration** | Well-defined instrument response | Variable ground coupling & response |
| **Primary Role** | Basin-wide monitoring & calibration | Dense, continuous near-shore array |

## 3. Scientific and Societal Objectives

- **Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR):** Significant reduction in offshore earthquake detection latency and rapid real-time verification of tsunami propagation for coastal communities.
- **Oceanography & Climate:** Basin-scale monitoring of deep-ocean thermal structure, thermohaline circulation, and absolute sea-level changes.
- **Global Seismology:** Extends the **Global Seismographic Network (GSN)** into deep ocean basins, filling major coverage gaps in global seismic tomography and plate-boundary microseismicity detection.

## 4. Active and Upcoming Projects

- **Testbeds & Demonstrators:**
    - **INSEA Wet Demo:** Deployed in the Mediterranean Sea testing sensor integration.
    - **Ocean Networks Canada (ONC) NEPTUNE Array:** Prototype subsea node testing.
- **Commercial Systems in Deployment:**
    - **TAMTAM Project:** Scheduled for installation connecting **New Caledonia to Vanuatu** across the active New Hebrides subduction zone. It incorporates an optical **bypass fiber** enabling long-range DAS to span the entire subsea cable alongside SMART repeater nodes.
    - **CAM-2 / Atlantic CAM Project:** A large-scale ring system connecting **mainland Portugal, the Azores, and Madeira** with **>20 smart repeater nodes** and **landing-station DAS** for regional earthquake and tsunami early warning.
- **Proposed Prospective Routes:**
    - **Trans-Antarctic Cable:** NSF-sponsored initiative exploring a route from **New Zealand to McMurdo Station (Antarctica)** to provide the first continuous seismic ray paths through the Southern Ocean.
    - **Arctic Circle Cable:** High-latitude route planned in collaboration with Charlotte Rowe (LANL) to monitor polar ocean basins.

## 5. Institutional and Operational Challenges

- **Telecom Industry Risk Aversion:** Ensuring sensor integration does not compromise primary commercial data transmission, repeater lifespan, or system reliability.
- **National Security & Data Governance:** National defense agencies require protocols to handle continuous acoustic and geophysical data streams without leaking sensitive maritime operational data.
- **Data Equity:** Developing open data standards that ensure critical hazard information reaches vulnerable island communities.

## Talk 3: Real-Time Seafloor Earthquake and Slow-Slip Monitoring (Nankai Trough)

- **Speaker & Affiliation:** Eichiro Araki. JAMSTEC

## 1. Geodynamic Motivation and System Vision

- **Tectonic Context:** The **Nankai Trough** megathrust subduction zone in southwest Japan generates recurring **M8–M9 earthquakes** on **100- to 200-year recurrence cycles**.
- **Observation Goals:** Real-time detection and characterization of both regular megathrust seismicity and slow earthquakes (shallow/deep episodic tremor and slow slip), while feeding automated **earthquake early warning (EEW)** and **tsunami warning** systems.
- **Monitoring Transition:** Integrating legacy cabled ocean-bottom seismometer networks with seafloor DAS, establishing a pathway toward fully distributed optical subsea observatories.

## 2. Dual-Array Infrastructure and Local Processing Pipeline

- **DONET 2 Array:** Comprises **14 broadband ocean-bottom seismometer (BBOBS) stations** sampled at **100 Hz**, distributed broadly across the 2D offshore subduction wedge. Telemetry is transmitted in **WIN32 format** (1-second data packets) via Yokohama.
- **Offshore Seafloor Cable (DAS):** Utilizes a **120-km-long legacy submarine telecommunications cable** extending offshore from Muroto. Interrogated via DAS at **500 Hz**, then downsampled to **100 Hz** with spatial decimation to **1 km channel intervals** to balance bandwidth and processing loads. Recorded in **HDF5 format** every 15 seconds.
- **Real-Time Edge Computing Pipeline:**
    - Deployed locally at the **Muroto landing station** to ensure operational autonomy during network severance.
    - Operates on a **2-minute sliding analysis window** delivering automated catalog updates **every 60 seconds** (<1 minute processing latency).

## 3. Dual Detection and Location Algorithms

1. **Phase Arrival-Based Association & Hypocenter Solver:**
    - Automated phase picking via **PhaseNet**.
    - Phase association across hybrid sensors via **GAMMA** (Gaussian Mixture Model Association).
    - Hypocenter determination benchmarked across two velocity models:
        - **Hypo:** Traditional 1D layered velocity structure.
        - **Hypo-Gen Nankai:** Deep-learning framework solving 3D travel times across complex subduction structures.
2. **Envelope Cross-Correlation Method (ECM):**
    - Continuous cross-correlation of **1 Hz – 8 Hz bandpassed waveform envelopes** across DAS channels and DONET stations.
    - Capable of detecting emerging, non-impulsive **seismic tremor** as well as regular earthquakes.

## 4. Experimental Results and Performance Analysis

- **Microseismic Detection:** ECM identified numerous ultra-small seismic events omitted from the national **Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA)** catalog.
- **Tremor Discrimination:** Analysis of event duration histograms during the April 2025 slow-slip sequence demonstrated that events with **durations exceeding 30 seconds** consistently correspond to tectonic seismic tremor.
- **1D vs. 3D Model Performance:** Hypocenter comparisons indicated that the 3D velocity model (**Hypo-Gen Nankai**) yielded no demonstrable accuracy improvement over the 1D model (**Hypo**) at this stage, largely due to phase identification errors.

## 5. Key Engineering and Seismological Challenges

- **Spurious ECM Array Mislocations:** Earthquakes originating _outside_ the array footprint were systematically mislocated into the _interior_ of the network. This artifact arises from coda-tail waveform envelope similarities producing false cross-correlation peaks.
- **P-Wave Attenuation and Masking on DAS:** Ambient seafloor DAS noise floors are substantially higher than cabled BBOBS. Direct P-wave arrivals could be identified on DAS channels in **only ~15% of events**, leaving **85%** without identifiable direct P phases.
- **P-to-S Conversion Errors:**
    - In thick unconsolidated seafloor sediments, upgoing direct P-waves undergo strong mode conversion into **P-to-S converted waves** at basement interfaces.
    - PhaseNet frequently misidentified these high-amplitude P-to-S converted arrivals as direct P-waves.
    - Resulting travel-time delays (reaching up to **1.5 seconds**) severely degraded focal depth resolution.
- **Remediation Strategy:** Analysis of 22 benchmark earthquakes with verified direct P picks is establishing spatial correction models for sediment-induced P-to-S time delays. These data will train next-generation neural network pickers that explicitly classify direct P and P-to-S converted phases.

## Talk 4: Environmental DAS for Precipitation Monitoring and Rain Gauging

- **Speaker & Affiliation:** Jonathan Ajo-Franklin (Rice University).

## 1. Scientific Background and Physical Principles

- **Historical Context:** Exploration geophone studies historically treated rain noise as destructive acoustic interference in seismic acquisition; raindrop impact spectra generate broadband acoustic energy extending up to **500 Hz** proportional to precipitation intensity.
- **Core Investigation:** Validating whether DAS on surface trenches and existing commercial telecom fiber can detect high-frequency raindrop signatures, establish quantitative linear rain-gauging metrics, and overcome urban environmental noise.

## 2. Phase 1: Rice Controlled Hydrogeophysics Test Site

- **Site Configuration:** An extensively instrumented test facility comprising **three monitoring wells** (PVC casing with optical fiber cemented behind casing), piezometers in shallow and deep aquifers, an on-site **automated meteorological station**, a **surface fiber-optic cable** buried at **~5 cm depth**, and a parallel array of **SmartSolo 5 Hz 3-component nodal seismometers**.
- **Acoustic Acquisition:** A Terra15 Treble interrogator logged continuously at **1 kHz sampling rate**, with a **1.6 m gauge length** and **0.82 m channel spacing**.
- **Severe Urban Noise Floor:** The site sits adjacent to the Texas Medical Center (100,000 daily commuters), the Rice campus, and a central power plant. Ambient noise regularly exceeds the **New High Noise Model (NHNM)**, dominated by low-frequency (<30 Hz) cultural and traffic noise.
- **Precipitation Acoustic Signature:**
    - In the time domain, rainfall appears as spatially uniform, incoherent random noise lacking propagating phase moveout.
    - In the frequency domain, rain generates a prominent, diffuse **high-frequency spectral signature between 30 Hz and 150–250 Hz**.
    - **Seismic vs. DAS:** SmartSolo nodes captured similar spectral shapes but were degraded by severe **electrical cross-talk** from neighboring power infrastructure; optical fiber was immune to electromagnetic interference.

## 3. Factors Influencing Sensitivity and Linearity

- **Precipitation Rate Linearity:** High-frequency power spectral density (**PSD > 30 Hz**) exhibited strong linear proportionality with calibrated rain-gauge precipitation rates.
- **Sensitivity Threshold:** Reliable detection was established down to **~4 mm/hr**; light drizzle fell below the ambient urban noise floor.
- **Foliage Canopy Attenuation:** Cables beneath open grass and bare ground exhibited strong acoustic coupling; conversely, sections beneath tree canopies suffered a **severe reduction in sensitivity** because overhead leaves intercepted the kinetic impact energy of falling raindrops. Remote-sensing canopy maps (e.g., LiDAR) could serve as correction factors.
- **Conduit vs. Direct Burial:** Direct burial in soil provided optimal acoustic transmission, whereas encasing fiber in rigid protective conduit significantly damped raindrop energy transfer.
- **Burial Depth Attenuation:**
    - At **5 cm depth**, signals were clear and sensitive.
    - At **0.5 m depth**, signal amplitude dropped by a factor of **2.5**.
    - At **1.5 m depth**, rainfall signatures were **completely undetectable**.

```
Depth (m)   Rainfall Acoustic Transmission
  0.0 m  ── [Raindrop Kinetic Impacts] ─────────────────────────
            │
  0.05 m ── [Direct-buried fiber: Strong, linear response (>30 Hz)]
            │ (amplitude drops by 2.5x)
  0.50 m ── [Noticeable high-frequency attenuation]
            │
  1.50 m ── [Signal completely attenuated / undetectable]
```

## 4. Distinguishing Rain from Cultural Noise (Spectral Slope)

- **False Triggers:** Simple high-frequency energy thresholds resulted in numerous false detections caused by passing heavy vehicles, trains, and construction machinery.
- **Spectral Slope Discriminant:**
    - **Vehicle/Cultural Noise:** High low-frequency energy that **rolls off steeply with increasing frequency** (strongly negative spectral slope).
    - **Raindrop Noise:** Uniform acoustic transfer producing a **flat or positive spectral slope** across the 30–150 Hz band.
    - Combining spectral slope filters with spatial continuity and moveout velocity constraints eliminated the majority of false triggers.

## 5. Phase 2: Downtown Houston Dark Fiber Telecom Demonstration

- **Network Setup:** A **12-km dark fiber route** running from the Rice campus through downtown Houston, interrogated using a Silixa QuantX at **10 m gauge length** and **5 m channel spacing**, calibrated against municipal personal weather stations.
- **Urban Installation Realities:** Conduits buried deeply beneath **2 to 4 feet of concrete** exhibited negligible acoustic sensitivity to rainfall.
- **Array "Sweet Spots":** Correlation across the 12 km route revealed **~30 distinct sensitive zones**. These sweet spots corresponded to **pull boxes, slack coils, and shallow surface utility controllers**, which act as acoustic diaphragms and resonators that efficiently couple raindrop vibrations into the fiber.
- **Future Work:** Developing machine-learning models to calibrate precipitation rates across non-uniform installations and integrating urban fiber arrays into municipal flood warning systems (published in _Seismica_, 2024/2026).
