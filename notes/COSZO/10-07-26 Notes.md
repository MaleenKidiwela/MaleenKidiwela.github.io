## 2. Conceptual Framework: Digital Twins vs. Complex Systems

A significant portion of the meeting addressed clarifying the theoretical and technical definition of a digital twin when applied to natural and social environments:

- **Two-Way Data Flow (Digital Twin vs. Shadow):**
    - A true **digital twin** requires a continuous **two-way flow of data**: real-time observational data feeding from the natural system into the computational models, and policy, mitigation, or operational feedback returning to influence the real-world system.
    - A **digital shadow** or **emulation** involves only a one-way ingest of system data for monitoring and offline consideration.
- **Complicated vs. Complex Systems:**
    - **Complicated Systems:** Systems where the overall operation equals the sum of the individual parts (e.g., an automobile or airplane, where the function of each spark plug or drivetrain component is deterministic). Standard engineering digital twins have operated successfully in this domain for over 15 years.
    - **Complex Systems:** Systems characterized by **emergent behavior** that cannot be predicted merely by summing individual components. Human actors within an ecosystem inherently introduce emergent behavior (e.g., an individual car is complicated, but traffic dynamics involving human drivers are complex).
- **Predictive Limits & Probabilistic Bounding:**
    - Traditional engineering models cannot deterministically predict human and ecological outcomes in complex systems.
    - Instead, modern approaches (pioneered by groups at NASA and the EU) utilize **bounded scenarios, linked probabilities, and statistical emulations** rather than exact deterministic predictions.
    - Examples discussed include assessing how a magnitude 9 earthquake impacts infrastructure differently in dry June versus late November when soils are saturated, or modeling human evacuation compliance during tsunami warnings.
- **Breaking Domain Silos:**
    - Domain experts typically model within isolated silos (circulation, weather, drainage, chemistry, biology, fisheries).
    - The primary architectural hurdle is formalizing the **inputs and outputs** that pass across silo boundaries in real time to capture inter-parameter interplay.

## 3. Workshop Structure & Proposed Agenda

The meeting reviewed a proposed **three-day format** hosted at the **UW Center for Urban Horticulture**:

### Day 1: Vision, Technical Landscape & Community Engagement

- **Morning Session (Open Plenary):**
    - Open to the broader University of Washington campus and public; targeting an audience of approximately **50 attendees** in a room with capacity for 180.
    - **Welcome Remarks:** Targeted for **Dean Joel** (College of the Environment).
    - **Opening Vision:** Presentation by **John Delaney** introducing urban sea systems and the digital twin concept.
    - **Anchor Keynote:** Presentation by **Rick Spinrad** (NOAA) focusing on urban marine systems and the **Blue Economy**.
    - **Nuts-and-Bolts Digital Twin Talks:** Presentations by industry engineers (e.g., Esri) showing practical examples of digital twin architecture to ground attendees in technical realities. Talks should be structured as focused 12–15 minute presentations followed by dedicated group discussion rather than rapid conference presentations.
- **Afternoon Session:**
    - Transition toward active participants: Short introductions and lightning pitches where attendees present their regional hazard challenges, domain models, or interests.
- **Evening Reception:**
    - On-site cocktail mixer at the Center for Urban Horticulture to promote informal interdisciplinary networking.
    - Attendees walk to dinner independently (e.g., Piatti) rather than a fully funded banquet.

### Day 2: Working Breakouts & Module Architecture

- **Morning Session (Breakout Groups):**
    - **Group 1 (Immediate Deliverables):** Scoping what modeling integrations can begin immediately with existing assets.
    - **Group 2 (Subcomponent / Module Modeling):** Defining modular components and evaluating what elements are amenable to subcomponent modeling.
    - **Group 3 (Inter-Silo Communication):** Defining data exchange protocols, physics, chemistry, and biological inputs/outputs between domain silos.
- **Afternoon Session:**
    - Synthesis of breakout outputs and buffer time for deep dives into specific module frameworks.
- **Evening:**
    - Hosted, paid dinner for the core committed working group.

### Day 3: Roadmap, Governance & Funding

- **Plenary Consensus:** Consolidate breakout findings into a shared 12-to-18-month roadmap.
- **Action Plan:** Establish concrete timelines, working group task lists, and designated leaders for each task.
- **Fundraising Strategy:** Align pitch targets for major tech companies (Microsoft, Amazon) and agency opportunities.
