

The papers point to one approach: start from rough candidate events, turn every one into a template, and run each template over **years** of data. The family is decided by what piles up over that long record, never from single events. Aden-Antoniów is the closest match to our situation. Nothing has been started; this is a proposal.

**What each paper does**

- **Aden-Antoniów 2024 (New Zealand):** the closest analogue. 12 permanent regional stations, LFEs at about 50 km depth.
    - **Seeds:** 580 rough candidates: 323 events from a tremor catalogue plus 257 from a back-projection detector.
    - **Templates:** each is 10 s long, S−2 to S+8 s, so it includes the coda. Each was run over 12 years.
    - **Clean-up:** seeds with fewer than 10 detections were dropped. The rest went through a deblur filter, clustering that keeps the 80% core, a restack and a second pass. Result: 36 families and 21k events.
    - **Threshold:** set with time-reversed templates. In our 23 Aug tremor test, reversed templates were too lenient.
- **Frank 2014 (Guerrero):** every peak of a beamformed network energy trace becomes an 8-s template on the 5 strongest stations, run over 2.5 years.
    - Noise families are removed because their detections are not clustered in time (fewer than 5% of gaps under 10 min).
    - Families are merged if they share at least 2/3 of detections and their stacks correlate at 2/3 or more.
    - It was a dense array (5–6 km spacing). The authors say beamforming only catches the strong events.
- **Lin 2024 and Münchmeyer 2024:** deep-learning pickers find single events, not families.
    - Münchmeyer's Cascadia catalogue holds about 5.6% of Bostock's events.
    - Both papers recommend using the picker only to supply varied templates, then template matching for completeness. Münchmeyer says template matching needs fewer stations.

**Why our tries failed:** every one decided from single events or short records.

- Pairwise grouping of single LFEs.
- Growing one template from the top detections, over 1 to 42 days.
- Grouping back-projection events by waveform.

A single LFE sits at about 2σ, so it only shows up once many are stacked. The papers get around this with time: a real seed piles up bursty repeats over years and a noise seed doesn't. We have not tested seeds over a multi-year record yet. Our September failures on permanent stations (3 of 77 families found) used Bostock's within-hour autocorrelation, which is a different method.

**Proposed recipe (Aden-style):**

1. **Seeds:** combine the PNSN tremor catalogue epicentres with their S moveout, the back-projection events we already have, and PNET-associated events.
2. **Templates:** 10–12 s, S−2 to S+8, three components, on the strongest 3–5 sites. Run each over the years in the 20 Hz caches, including the periods between ETS episodes.
3. **Threshold:** for each template and day, set by stations shifted to other days, since reversed templates were too lenient here.
4. **Screens (kept as columns, not cuts):** number of detections, clustering in time, timing against tremor, and high-frequency deficit.
5. **Merge, then refine:** merge with the 2/3 rule, then deblur, keep the 80% core, restack and run a second pass.
6. **Check first:** test on Vancouver Island with the catalogue withheld. It should recover the 30 richest families before we try it on the sparse Washington and south-branch stations.

The main risk is seed supply. Aden kept only 36 of 580 seeds (about 6%), and Merlin estimates 10–20% of our single-event seeds would pass. With thousands of seeds that should still be enough.
