# Mapping $\delta\beta(x_0,y_0)$ on the Fault Plane from Many LFE-Family / Station Coda Pairs

**Companion to "Coda-Wave Sensitivity Kernel for a Deep Repeating Source and a Surface Receiver."** That document gives the volumetric kernel $K(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{s},\mathbf{r},t)$; this one assembles all $(\text{LFE family}, \text{station}, \text{lapse window})$ observations into a single inverse problem whose model is the relative shear-velocity change *as a function of position on the fault plane*, $m(\xi)=\delta\beta/\beta\,(x_0,y_0)$.

---

## 0. The idea in one paragraph

Each LFE family is a fixed repeating source sitting **on the fault**, at fault coordinate $\xi_a=(x_{0a},y_{0a})$. The travel-time kernel for that family recorded at any surface station has its **deep lobe pinned at $\xi_a$** — i.e. its strongest sensitivity is to the velocity change of the family's *own* fault patch. So family $a$ "self-images" patch $\xi_a$, with weaker leakage to neighboring patches set by the deep-lobe width, plus off-fault sensitivity along the up-going tube and under the station. Tile the fault with the families you have, add every station and lapse window as a redundant look, and the result is a fault-plane tomography with a near-diagonal core (each family ↔ its patch) plus a controllable off-fault nuisance space that the network redundancy lets you suppress.

---

## 1. Indices, model, and the one modeling choice

| Index / symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $a=1,\dots,A$ | LFE family; source on the fault at 3-D position $\mathbf{X}_a=\mathbf{X}(\xi_a)$, fault coord $\xi_a=(x_{0a},y_{0a})$ |
| $b=1,\dots,B$ | surface station at $\mathbf{R}_b$ |
| $i=1,\dots,I$ | lapse-time window centre $t_i$ |
| $\xi=(x_0,y_0)$ | intrinsic coordinate on the (dipping, possibly curved) fault surface $\Sigma$; embedding $\mathbf{X}(\xi)$ |
| $k=1,\dots,K$ | fault-mesh node; the family locations $\{\xi_a\}$ are a subset of the nodes $\{\xi_k\}$ |
| $h(\xi)$ | fault-zone thickness (across-fault extent over which $\delta\beta/\beta$ is coherent) |
| $A_k$ | area of fault patch $k$ |
| $m_k=\dfrac{\delta\beta}{\beta}(\xi_k)$ | **target model**: relative shear-velocity change on fault patch $k$ |

**The modeling choice (state it, defend it, price it).** We assume the velocity changes we are imaging are *localized to the fault zone*, so the volumetric field collapses onto the surface $\Sigma$. This is justified physically — fault zones concentrate the fluids, pore-pressure transients, and damage/healing that move $\beta$ — and geometrically — the deep lobe is pinned to $\Sigma$ by the on-fault source. Its **cost** is quantified in §2: anything that changes off the fault (shallow site hydrology, bulk crustal change) projects onto the data through the tube and surface lobe and must be carried as nuisance (§4) or it biases $\hat{m}$.

## 2. From the 3-D kernel to a fault-plane kernel

Start from the forward relation in the companion document,
$\delta\tau/t=-\int_V K(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{s},\mathbf{r},t)\,(\delta\beta/\beta)\,d^3x$, with $\int_V K\,d^3x=1$.

**Step 1 — Thin-zone collapse.** If $\delta\beta/\beta$ is confined to a thin zone of thickness $h(\xi)$ about $\Sigma$ and varies slowly across it, the volume integral reduces to a surface integral:

$$
\frac{\delta\tau}{t}\Big|_{a,b,i}
=-\int_\Sigma \underbrace{K\big(\mathbf{X}(\xi);\mathbf{X}_a,\mathbf{R}_b,t_i\big)\,h(\xi)}_{\displaystyle \kappa_{abi}(\xi)\ \ [\text{area}^{-1}]}\;\frac{\delta\beta}{\beta}(\xi)\,dA(\xi)\;+\;e^{\text{off}}_{abi}.
\tag{1}
$$

$\kappa_{abi}(\xi)$ is the **effective 2-D (fault-plane) kernel**. The term $e^{\text{off}}_{abi}=-\int_{V\setminus\Sigma}K\,(\delta\beta/\beta)\,d^3x$ is the off-fault contribution (§4).

**Step 2 — Captured-sensitivity fraction.** Because $\int_V K\,d^3x=1$ but only the fault slice is kept,

$$
\eta_{abi}\equiv\int_\Sigma \kappa_{abi}(\xi)\,dA(\xi)\;\le\;1
\tag{2}
$$

is the fraction of the coda's sensitivity that actually lies on the fault for that pair. $\eta$ is largest for short lapse times (tight deep lobe on $\Sigma$) and shrinks as the field diffuses into the off-fault bulk and toward the station. **Reporting $\eta_{abi}$ is the honest measure of how fault-confined each datum is** — low-$\eta$ pairs are dominated by off-fault paths and should be down-weighted.

> *Physical note:* the deep lobe sits on the fault by construction (on-fault source), so $\kappa_{abi}(\xi)$ peaks sharply at $\xi=\xi_a$. The peak width is the deep-lobe radius $\sim\sqrt{2D\,t_i}$ in the diffusive regime (or $\sim\ell^\*$), which sets the fault-plane resolution length (§6).

## 3. Discretized forward operator over all pairs

Discretize (1) on the fault mesh. A single datum is

$$
d_{abi}\equiv\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{\tau}\right)_{abi}
=\sum_{k=1}^{K} G_{(abi),k}\,m_k+e^{\text{off}}_{abi}+\varepsilon_{abi},
\qquad
\boxed{\,G_{(abi),k}=-\,\kappa_{abi}(\xi_k)\,A_k=-\,K(\mathbf{X}_k;\mathbf{X}_a,\mathbf{R}_b,t_i)\,h_k A_k\,}.
\tag{3}
$$

Stack all valid triples $(a,b,i)$ into the data vector $\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{R}^N$ ($N=\sum_a B_a I_a$, missing pairs simply omitted), the fault model $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}^K$, and the design matrix $\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times K}$:

$$
\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}^{\text{off}}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}.
\tag{4}
$$

**Structure of $\mathbf{G}$ — the near-diagonal core.** Because $\kappa_{abi}$ peaks at $\xi_a$, the rows belonging to family $a$ load most heavily onto the model node $k(a)$ co-located with the source. Ordering nodes so that family nodes come first, $\mathbf{G}$ is dominated by a (block-)diagonal "self-imaging" part plus off-diagonal leakage to neighboring patches:

$$
d_{abi}\approx \underbrace{-\,\kappa^{\text{self}}_{abi}A_{k(a)}\,m_{k(a)}}_{\text{own patch}}
\;\underbrace{-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(a)}\kappa_{abi}(\xi_k)A_k\,m_k}_{\text{neighbour leakage}}
\;+\;e^{\text{off}}_{abi}+\varepsilon_{abi}.
\tag{5}
$$

This is what makes the problem well-conditioned where families are dense: every patch hosting a family is directly illuminated by its own source.

---

## 4. Off-fault nuisance and the bipartite (two-way) structure

The off-fault term $e^{\text{off}}_{abi}$ has two physically distinct, *separable* parts that the all-pairs design lets you remove:

- a **site term** $c_b$: a shallow velocity change near station $b$ (e.g. groundwater), sampled by the surface lobe — depends on $b$, **not** on the source;
- a **path/bulk term** $p_{abi}$: a smooth change in the off-fault crust along the $a\!\to\!b$ tube.

Write the augmented forward model

$$
\boxed{\;\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{G}_f\,\mathbf{m}_f+\mathbf{G}_s\,\mathbf{m}_s+\mathbf{G}_p\,\mathbf{m}_p+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\;}
\tag{6}
$$

where $\mathbf{m}_f$ is the fault map (target), $\mathbf{m}_s=\{c_b\}$ the per-station site terms ($\mathbf{G}_s$ built from the surface-lobe weights), and $\mathbf{m}_p$ a coarse 3-D off-fault field ($\mathbf{G}_p$ from the tube weights).

**Why the network makes this identifiable.** Index the data by the pair $(a,b)$. To leading order the datum is *additive* in a source-side (fault) effect that is a function of $a$ and a receiver-side (site) effect that is a function of $b$:

$$
d_{abi}\;\approx\;\alpha_{abi}\,m_{k(a)}\;+\;\beta_{abi}\,c_b\;+\;(\text{path}).
\tag{7}
$$

This is a **two-way / bipartite structure** (a row effect per fault patch, a column effect per station). Two consequences you can use directly:

1. **Common-mode over stations isolates the fault.** Averaging a family's data over all its stations and lapse windows,
$$
\langle d\rangle_a=\frac{1}{B_aI_a}\sum_{b,i}d_{abi}\;\approx\;\langle\alpha\rangle_a\,m_{k(a)}+\langle\beta\,c\rangle_a,
$$
and if site changes are zero-mean across the network the second term averages out, giving a quick robust estimate $\hat m_{k(a)}\approx-\langle d\rangle_a/(\langle\kappa^{\text{self}}\rangle_a A_{k(a)})$. A *true* fault change is the part of family $a$'s signal that is **coherent across all stations recording it**.
2. **Common-mode over families isolates the site.** Averaging over all families recorded at station $b$ isolates $c_b$. These two marginals are the practical first pass; the joint inversion (§5) refines them and handles the non-zero-mean case.

> If site changes are *not* zero-mean (a regional shallow transient), the marginal estimate is biased — that is exactly when you must keep $\mathbf{m}_s$ explicit in (6) rather than relying on the marginal.

## 5. The overall inverse problem

Solve (6) as a single regularized least-squares (or Bayesian) problem:

$$
\boxed{\;
[\hat{\mathbf{m}}_f,\hat{\mathbf{m}}_s,\hat{\mathbf{m}}_p]
=\arg\min\;
\big\|\mathbf{W}^{1/2}\!\left(\mathbf{G}_f\mathbf{m}_f+\mathbf{G}_s\mathbf{m}_s+\mathbf{G}_p\mathbf{m}_p-\mathbf{d}\right)\big\|^2
+\lambda_f\big\|\mathbf{L}_\Sigma\mathbf{m}_f\big\|^2
+\lambda_s\|\mathbf{m}_s\|^2
+\lambda_p\big\|\mathbf{L}_3\mathbf{m}_p\big\|^2
\;}
\tag{8}
$$

- $\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{C}_d^{-1}$ data weights; set entry $(abi)$ from the CWI measurement uncertainty and **scale by $\eta_{abi}$** (eq. 2) so low-fault-sensitivity pairs count less.
- $\mathbf{L}_\Sigma$ is the **surface Laplacian on the fault mesh**, optionally anisotropic (smoother along strike than dip) to encode fault fabric. This couples patches with no family of their own to their neighbours, so the fault map is defined on the full mesh, not just at family nodes.
- $\lambda_s$ damps the site terms; $\mathbf{L}_3$ smooths the coarse path field.

Bayesian/Gaussian form with model covariance $\mathbf{C}_m=\mathrm{blkdiag}(\mathbf{C}_f,\mathbf{C}_s,\mathbf{C}_p)$ and full design $\mathbf{G}=[\mathbf{G}_f\,\mathbf{G}_s\,\mathbf{G}_p]$:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{m}}=\big(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\mathbf{C}_d^{-1}\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{C}_m^{-1}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\mathbf{C}_d^{-1}\mathbf{d},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{\hat m}=\big(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\mathbf{C}_d^{-1}\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{C}_m^{-1}\big)^{-1},
\qquad
\mathbf{R}=\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{\hat m}\,\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\mathbf{C}_d^{-1}\mathbf{G}.
\tag{9}
$$

Solve with LSQR/CGLS for large $K$. The fault-block of $\mathbf{R}$ gives the spatial resolution of $\hat{\mathbf{m}}_f$ (§6); the cross-blocks of $\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{\hat m}$ tell you how much fault↔site↔path trade-off remains given your station geometry (Aster et al., 2018; Tarantola, 2005).

## 6. Resolution and experiment design on the fault

- **Fault-plane resolution length:**
$$
L_{\text{res}}(\xi)\approx\max\!\big(\underbrace{\sqrt{2D\,t_{\min}}}_{\text{deep-lobe radius}},\;\underbrace{\Delta_{\text{LFE}}(\xi)}_{\text{family spacing}}\big).
\tag{10}
$$
Two levers: denser LFE families tighten $\Delta_{\text{LFE}}$; **earlier lapse windows tighten the deep lobe.** Note this is the opposite of surface–surface monitoring, where you push to *late* coda to reach depth: here the source is already at depth, so the earliest usable S-coda gives the sharpest fault image. Use $Q_i$ only to set how early-to-late you can go before the coda drops into noise — it does not change kernel shape (companion doc, §3).
- **Azimuthal station coverage per family** decides whether the on-fault patch separates from the up-going tube. A single station cannot tell "change at $\xi_a$" from "change anywhere along my tube"; an azimuthal ring of stations sharing family $a$ pins the change to the common deep lobe.
- **Run point-spread / checkerboard tests** with synthetic fault patterns through the real $\mathbf{G}$ to map where the family+station layout actually resolves the fault and where the null space (off-fault leakage) dominates.

---

## 7. Time-lapse (4-D) and the source-migration channel

**4-D map.** Each family repeats, so every observable is a time series. Replace the scalar measurement by epochs $T$ (reference vs current, or a sliding stack):
$$
d_{abi}(T)=\sum_k G_{(abi),k}\,m_k(T)+\dots,\qquad m_k(T)=\frac{\delta\beta}{\beta}(\xi_k,T).
$$
Invert each epoch (or jointly with temporal smoothing $\|\partial_T \mathbf{m}_f\|^2$) to get $\delta\beta/\beta(x_0,y_0,T)$ across the slab — e.g. through an ETS cycle. This is the deliverable: a movie of shear-velocity change on the plate interface.

**Source migration as a second-moment channel.** From the companion document (§8), a source displacement $\boldsymbol{\delta}_a$ of family $a$ produces *no* coherent stretch but a lapse-time-independent variance $\sigma_{\tau,a}^2=\delta_a^2/(3\beta^2)$, common across all stations recording $a$. Add a per-family migration parameter estimated from the incoherent (variance) channel rather than the stretch channel; it is orthogonal to $\mathbf{m}_f$ (first moment, coherent, $\propto t$ vs. second moment, incoherent, flat in $t$) so it does not alias onto the fault velocity map. This gives you, alongside $\delta\beta/\beta(x_0,y_0,T)$, a map of **where the LFE sources themselves are moving** — a second fault-process observable for free.

---

## 8. Algorithm

1. **Geometry & medium.** Place each family on the fault mesh $\mathbf{X}(\xi_a)$; estimate $\beta,\ell^\*$ (so $D=\beta\ell^\*/3$), $Q_i$, and a fault-zone thickness prior $h(\xi)$.
2. **Kernels.** For every valid $(a,b,i)$ compute the volumetric kernel (companion eq. 13), restrict to $\Sigma$, multiply by $h_k$ → effective 2-D kernel $\kappa_{abi}(\xi_k)$; record the captured-sensitivity fraction $\eta_{abi}$ (eq. 2). Use the single-scattering ellipsoid / RTE kernel for the earliest windows and within $\ell^\*$ of stations.
3. **Data.** Per $(a,b,i,T)$ measure the coherent stretch $(\delta\tau/\tau)$ (→ $\mathbf{d}$) and the residual variance $\sigma_\tau$ (→ migration channel).
4. **Assemble** $\mathbf{G}=[\mathbf{G}_f\,\mathbf{G}_s\,\mathbf{G}_p]$ (eq. 3, 6); weight by $\mathbf{C}_d^{-1}$ scaled by $\eta_{abi}$.
5. **Quick look:** network-common marginals (eq. of §4) for a regularization-free first map and a fault-vs-site sanity check.
6. **Invert** (8)/(9) with the fault-surface Laplacian; tune $\lambda_f,\lambda_s,\lambda_p$ by L-curve / cross-validation.
7. **Resolve & validate:** resolution matrix, point-spread tests, and a full-wavefield synthetic with a planted on-fault patch to confirm $\hat{\mathbf{m}}_f$ recovers it without off-fault smearing.
8. **Iterate over epochs** $T$ for the 4-D fault map; co-estimate per-family migration $\delta_a$ from the variance channel.
