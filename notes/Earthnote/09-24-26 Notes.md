# The published Cascadia LFE catalogues — what each one is, and whether we are reproducing it

**Gloss of terms used throughout.** *LFE* = low-frequency earthquake, a very small earthquake (magnitude ~1–2) that repeats in the same spot thousands of times and is depleted in high frequencies. *Family* = one such spot; all the events in a family have the same waveform. *Template* = the average waveform of a family, built by stacking many detections. *Network autocorrelation* = sliding every short window of a day's data against every other window and summing across stations, to find pairs that look identical — this is how families are *discovered* with no prior knowledge. *Matched filter* / *network cross-correlation* = sliding a known template through the data to find more copies — this is how a family is *extended* once you have it. *MAD* = median absolute deviation, a robust measure of scatter; "8×MAD" means a detection is logged when the correlation exceeds 8 of those units above the median. *Declustering* = keeping only the strongest detection in a short time interval so one event is not logged twice. Ordered geographically, north to south.

---

## 1. Royer & Bostock 2014 — northern Vancouver Island (NVI)

**What they found.** 54 initial templates, reduced to a final suite of **47** ("Our final suites of templates number 122, 54 and 47 for the NW, SW and NVI datasets", §3.3), from **4267 initial detections** (§3.1). Data span two short tremor episodes only: **5–11 Sept 2006** and **13–18 June 2007**, "each episode lasting under 1 week" (§2.2). Depths: "hypocentres fall between **33 and 39 km**" (§5). Each template has "100's to 1000's of independent detections" (§3.3). **They located them — yes, all of them**, first with **Hyp2000**, then relocated with **hypoDD**; "**91 %** … of the original events remain in hypoDD solutions for the NVI … dataset" (§5). Hyp2000 nominal errors 1.8 km horizontal, 2.16 km vertical.

**Method in one paragraph.** A 27-station portable POLARIS deployment on two perpendicular arms (§2.2); network autocorrelation used "**different combinations of 7 of the 10 three-component stations VI10, VW03, VI11, VW02, VI52, VI53, VI05, VI08, VI06, VI04**" (§3.1). All data cut into 24-hour files, **1–8 Hz, 40 samples per second** (§3); inside each hour, **15-second windows lagged by 0.5 s** (7170 windows per hour) correlated against every other window on each station-component, summed across all stations and components; detection when the sum exceeds **8 × MAD** of that hour (§3.1); then the detections were "culled to exclude overlapping detections and retain only those with high SNR". Detections were turned into families by joining every channel into one "**super** trace", cross-correlating all such traces, and running a **hierarchical cluster analysis** on the resulting similarity matrix (§3.2). Templates were then improved by iterating network cross-correlation with "**subsets of 7 to 10 high SNR, three-component stations**", now thresholded at **8 × MAD over a 24-hour period** (note: the MAD window changes from 1 hour to 24 hours between the two stages), restacking after each pass, for "several iterations" (§3.3).

**What they released.** Waveforms via IRIS/GSC. **No catalogue.** The supplementary material is one table of hypoDD settings and four figures — no family list, no detection times. SILENT.

**Are we using their method? NO.** Our chain uses **6-second windows** (`lfe_array_discovery/scripts/k_auto.py:42`) where they used 15; **20 samples per second** (`k_common.py:28`) where they used 40; and **connected-component graph grouping** (`k_group.py:51-65,96-99`) where they used hierarchical clustering. We have also never run the NVI stations.

**Status.** **Not attempted.** No NVI run exists.

**To reproduce from here.** Download the 10 named POLARIS NVI stations for the two 2006/2007 tremor weeks; run at 15 s / 40 sps / 1–8 Hz; score against 47 final templates at 33–39 km depth.

## 2. Bostock et al. 2012 — southern Vancouver Island (SVI) — *the catalogue we reproduced*

**What they found.** "An initial suite of **5775 LFEs** detected in 2004 and 2005 at a select set of 7 stations is grouped into **140 families**" (Abstract; §2.1 ¶6). 46 tremor days analysed. "Most templates comprise between **1000–5000 independent detections** made over the 8 ETS episodes" (§2.2 ¶7). "The LFEs define a relatively tight surface **between 27 and 37 km depth**" (§3 ¶9). **They located them — yes, all 140**, with **hypoinverse** [Klein 2002], from "averages of approximately **16 P-times and 22 S-times** per template" (§3 ¶8). They deliberately did *not* deduplicate: "Although some of the 140 composite waveform templates exhibit overlap in contributing detections, we will consider each template as representing a distinct LFE location" (§3 ¶8).

**Method in one paragraph — note how it differs from §1 above, same authors, one year earlier.** Discovery used the 2004 (July 4–26) and 2005 (Sept 3–25) ETS episodes and the POLARIS/GSC "anchor" line "**TWBB, TSJB, TWKB, MGCB, KELB**, and permanent stations **PFB, LZB and PGC**" with "5–10 km station spacing" (§2 ¶4) — the paper lists eight codes but calls them "the 7 three-component anchor stations" (§2.1 ¶5), an inconsistency in the paper itself. Band **1–10 Hz** (not 1–8), **40 sps**, 24-hour files. Windows were **6 seconds with 0.5 s overlap**, 7188 per hour, each correlated against every other window in the same hour; summed over stations and components; detection at **8 × MAD** (§2.1 ¶5). Grouping was "a **hierarchical cluster analysis** [Rowe et al. 2002] based on correlation coefficients of composite wave trains" (§2.1 ¶6). Extension used "subset templates comprising **6 or 7 stations**", again at 8 × MAD, alternating detection and restacking; "**After 5 such iterations** neither template SNR nor numbers of detections continued to show significant improvement" (§2.2 ¶7). No declustering rule is published.

**What they released.** Waveform access acknowledgement only. **No catalogue in the paper** — SILENT. (The 130-family successor set from Bostock et al. 2015 is what our project works from; that paper is also SILENT on release.)

**Are we using their method? PARTLY.**
- window 6 s vs their 6 s — **matches** (`k_auto.py:42`)
- threshold 8 × MAD vs their 8 × MAD — **matches** (`k_auto.py:45`)
- band **1–8 Hz vs their 1–10 Hz** (`d_equiv.py:5-8`)
- sample rate **20 sps vs their 40 sps** (`k_common.py:28`)
- stations: our 11-station `CLEAN11` = GLBC, TWKB, MGCB, TSJB, TWBB, PGC, SILB, SSIB, LCBC, B003, B005 (`k_common.py:49`), and the queues run 15 (`k_repro3_queue.sh:13`) — **only 5 of their 8 anchors are in it** (TWKB, MGCB, TSJB, TWBB, PGC; KELB, PFB and LZB are missing from CLEAN11, LZB is added in the 15-station set)
- grouping **connected components vs their hierarchical clustering** (`k_group.py:51-65`)
- iterations **3 vs their 5** (`k_grow.py:234`)
- declustering **4 s, which we imported from Bostock et al. 2015** (`k_auto.py:46`) where this paper publishes no rule at all

**Status. REPRODUCED — the project's anchor result.** Blind on the 2005 array bed with 15 stations: **58 of 96** open ("kept") families and **22 of 30** sealed hold-out families rediscovered, against ceilings of 89 and 29 respectively (`lfe_array_discovery/results/REPRO_BOSTOCK.md`, `NORTH_DISCOVERY_SUMMARY.md`). The same chain on permanent-station beds discovers only 3/77 (2012) and 5/60 (2010) — dense arrays find families, permanent networks only run them.

**To reproduce more exactly from here.** Move the cache to 40 sps and 1–10 Hz, add KELB/PFB, swap connected components for hierarchical clustering, and raise iterations to 5.

## 3. Chestler & Creager 2017 — Olympic Peninsula

**What they found.** "Initially, we found **57 LFE families** beneath the Olympic Peninsula …, each with **250–1700 detections** … There are **34,479 LFEs** in total" (§3.1); after removing duplicate families, "our final catalog includes … **32,387 unique LFEs sorted into 43 families**" (§3.1). Moments span "M0 ≈ 1.4 × 10^10 to 1.9 × 10^12 N m (**Mw = 0.7–2.1**)" (§3.2). Time span: the Array of Arrays deployment, June 2009 – Sept 2010 and Aug 2011. **They located them — yes, all 43**: "we perform a **grid search using the 3D velocity model from Preston et al. [2003]** to locate each LFE family", then "improve relative locations between nearby families using the **double difference method of Waldhauser and Ellsworth [2000]**" (§2.1). The paper does not print a depth range in the text (cross-section only) — SILENT.

**Method in one paragraph — a completely different recipe from the two above.** They did not autocorrelate raw station records. They started from Ghosh et al.'s array **beams**: eight 1-km arrays of 10–20 three-component stations, **vertical component only, 5–9 Hz**, 1-minute windows with 50 % overlap, delay-and-sum beamforming over a slowness grid (§2.1). On each beam they took peaks "**larger than 18 times the median absolute deviation**", spread each peak into a 2-second boxcar, stacked the boxcars across arrays, and used the peaks of that as candidate LFEs. Only then did they autocorrelate: "**15 s windows** of three-component data, band-passed between **2 and 8 Hz**", using **three stations per array**, stacked "using an **nth-root stack where n = 2**", with a detection bar of "**45 times the MAD**" (§2.1) — a far higher bar on a different statistic than the 8 × MAD used by Bostock and Royer. Templates were built from "**up to 150** of the time windows that correlate best", refined over "**three iterations**", and family membership used a correlation threshold that slides with how many stations were live: **0.05 / 0.1 / 0.14 / 0.15 / 0.2** for <6, 7–9, 10–12, 13–15, >15 stations. Duplicate families were removed when they shared more than half their detections ("When two LFE families have **Rcom > 0.5**, the family with the lower median correlation coefficient is removed", §2.2).

**What they released.** "All the waveforms from the Array of Arrays and CAFE experiments can be found at the IRIS Data Management Center" (Acknowledgments). **Catalogue: SILENT** — only Table S1 in the supporting information (characteristic moment per family). No detection times, no coordinates, in the paper.

**Are we using their method? NO.** We do build array beams (`lfe_array_discovery/scripts/o_beam.py:49-52`, 1-minute windows, 50 % overlap, slowness grid — faithful), but every step after that is the Bostock recipe, not Chestler's:
- **no 18 × MAD beam-peak stage and no boxcar cross-array stack at all**
- autocorrelation window **6 s vs their 15 s** (`o_auto.py` imports `k_auto.WIN_S`, `k_auto.py:42`)
- band **1–8 Hz vs their 2–8 Hz** for the autocorrelation, and **5–8 vs their 5–9 Hz** for the beam (a documented cache limitation, `o_beam.py:18-20`)
- **linear stack at 8 × MAD vs their 2nd-root stack at 45 × MAD** (`k_auto.py:45`)
- template stack **200 vs their 150** (`k_grow.py:39`); iterations 3 vs their 3 — matches
- **no station-count-dependent family cc ladder, and no Rcom > 0.5 dedupe** in the running chain
- location: **Crosson 1-D grid search with VP/VS = 1.78** (`uu_common.py:177,220`) plus our own damped least-squares double-difference (`uu_dd.py:30-39`) vs their **Preston 3-D model with VP/VS = √3 ≈ 1.732** and Waldhauser–Ellsworth

**Status. A SPATIAL MATCH ONLY, and the phrasing matters.** Our 2010 Olympic arrays-as-arrays run produced 1,479 candidate families of which 788 located; **61 of 69** published Olympic coordinates have one of ours within 10 km, against **3 of 69** for the whole-chain site-shift null (`lfe_array_discovery/results/WA_LOCATION_REBUILD.md` §9). Two honest caveats: (a) those 69 coordinates are *not* Chestler's 43 — they are a 69-row coordinate file recovered from a public repository whose provenance we could not confirm, and our own file name says so (`codex-LFE/results/olympic_reference_discovery_v1/derived_coordinate_rows_not_a_catalog.csv`); (b) "61 of 69 coordinates have a neighbour within 10 km among our 788" is a *coverage* statement, not 61 identified families. An earlier, different comparison on the same 2010 bed was outright negative: our candidates sat *farther* from those coordinates than random draws (`WA_2010_RESULTS.md` §5d, "fraction of random draws at least as close: **1.000**").

**To reproduce from here.** Implement the missing front end — 18 × MAD beam peaks, boxcar cross-array stack, 15-s 2–8 Hz autocorrelation, 2nd-root stack, 45 × MAD — then the Rcom > 0.5 dedupe, and score against 57→43 families and 32,387 LFEs.

## 4. Royer & Bostock 2014 — northwest Washington (NW), eastern flank of the Olympics

**What they found.** "**4915** … initial detections" → "**224** … initial templates" → a final suite of "**122**" templates (§3.1–3.3). Data from major tremor episodes in 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010 and 2011, autocorrelated on four of them: **17–30 Jan 2007, 1–23 May 2008, 7–24 Aug 2010, 4–23 Aug 2011** (§3.1). Depths: "those below Washington state fall between **31 and 46 km** depth (NW)" (§5). **They located them — yes.** Hyp2000 for all, then hypoDD, which retained "**77 %** of the original events" for NW (§5). Hyp2000 nominal errors **2.0 km horizontal, 1.3 km vertical**.

**Method in one paragraph.** The station pool was "**25 stations** skirting the eastern flanks of the Olympic Peninsula to the west of Puget Sound" (§2.1), mostly from the CAFE Flexible Array. Network autocorrelation used exactly **seven named three-component stations: B001, SQM, BS11, W020, W040, GNW and PL11** (§3.1). Everything else is as in §1 above: 24-hour files, **1–8 Hz, 40 sps**, **15-second windows lagged 0.5 s**, **8 × MAD over the hour**, cull overlapping and low-SNR detections, hierarchical clustering of super-trace similarity into 224 initial templates, then iterated network cross-correlation with 7–10 high-SNR 3-C stations at **8 × MAD over 24 hours**, converging to 122.

**What they released.** **Nothing.** No catalogue, no coordinates, no detection times — SILENT. Their *positive claim* (families exist here, on the interface, at 31–46 km) is nonetheless testable on our side by existence.

**Are we using their method? NO — and this is the failure the project already hit.**
- window **6 s vs their 15 s** (`k_auto.py:42`)
- sample rate **20 sps vs their 40 sps** (`k_common.py:28`)
- stations: **52 probe-admitted stations** chosen by our own quiet-day contrast probe (`lfe_array_discovery/results/site_probe_2008.csv`, selection at `n_common.py:171-208`) **vs their 7 named**. Worse, **three of their seven are not in our set at all** — SQM, GNW and W020 are absent (GNW was explicitly rejected by our probe, `n_report.py:200`); only B001, BS11, PL11 and W040 survive.
- grouping **connected components vs their hierarchical clustering** (`k_group.py:51-65`)
- location **Crosson 1-D + our own LSQR double-difference vs their Hyp2000 + hypoDD** (`uu_locate.py:37-41`, `uu_dd.py:30-39`); their hypoDD settings are published in Table S1 (IDAT 3, five blocks of 8 iterations = 40, DAMP 50/50/40/40/40) and we have never used them.

**Status. FAILED — and the failure is now attributed to the method, not to Washington.** The 51-station Puget/S-Olympic 2008 run found nothing usable: the pair stage fired as often on a quiet day as inside the ETS (tremor/quiet ratio **0.89**, `WA_2008Q_RESULTS.md` intro) against 11× on the Vancouver Island bed where the same code reproduces Bostock. The project plan already records this as "BUG REPORTS against our code, not evidence about Washington" (`notes/MARGIN_CATALOG_PLAN_2026-09-24.md`). The present document identifies the bug concretely: **the run used Bostock's 6-second Vancouver Island window and our own 52-station probe set, not Royer & Bostock's 15-second window and their seven named stations.**

**To reproduce from here.** Re-cache the four NW tremor episodes at 40 sps, run at 15 s / 1–8 Hz on exactly B001, SQM, BS11, W020, W040, GNW, PL11 (downloading SQM, GNW, W020, which we do not have), group by hierarchical clustering, then extend with 7–10-station subsets at 24-hour MAD. Reference lines: 4915 detections, 224 initial templates, 122 final, depths 31–46 km.

## 6. Thomas & Bostock 2015 — central Oregon (~44°N)

**What they found.** "a total of **18 families** and over **15,000** individual LFE detections" (§3; the abstract says "nearly 16 thousand events"). Scan span: "**46 days of continuous data from August 3 through September 17, 2009**" (§2). **They located them — only partly.** "Of these 18, we were unable to locate **five** families because the templates were not of sufficient quality to accurately pick arrivals" (§3), leaving **13 locatable** (Fig. 3 caption). Of those 13, "most locations have poor depth control so we **fix their depths to the plate interface**"; only **four** have free depths, "ranging from **31 to 34 km**" with the Williams et al. (2011) model, "**increase[d] by an average of 4 km**" with the PNSN O0 model (§3). Software: **HYPOINVERSE**. Falsifiable extra: the slip front reached the northernmost families "on **August 30th** and propagates south at a rate of **5 km/day**" (§3).

**Method in one paragraph — and a published negative result we must respect.** They first tried exactly the Bostock/Royer approach and it failed outright: "We applied network autocorrelation to 3 days of continuous data (09/03–09/05) … the network autocorrelation method **failed to identify a single LFE template**. This is likely due to the station spacing (approximately 30 km on average)" (§2). So they switched to a **cross-station** method (Savard & Bostock 2014) applied to just **three** stations, "**ALP0, ALS0, and BWO0** … due to their generally high signal-to-noise ratios and proximity to the 2009 SSE" (§2). Data were **1–10 Hz, 40 sps, 24-hour files**; network cross-correlation at **8 × MAD**; **phase-weighted stacking on the intermediate iterations** (a stacking scheme that up-weights parts of the stack that agree in phase — neither Bostock 2012 nor Royer 2014 uses it), iterating "until no additional detections are registered (generally **10–15 iterations**)", with stations added as coherent energy appeared. A template needed "a **minimum of 100 detections in the 3-day window**". Families were deduplicated so that "**no family shares more than 10 % of detection times**", matched within **5 s**. The autocorrelation/cross-correlation window length is **SILENT** — they say only that they "experimented with window durations".

**What they released.** IRIS waveform acknowledgement only. **Catalogue: SILENT.**

**Are we using their method? NO.** We have no cross-station detector at all; our chain is the network-autocorrelation one that this paper reports **does not work in Oregon**. Specific differences: **6 s window vs SILENT** (`k_auto.py:42`); **20 sps vs their 40** (`k_common.py:28`); **1–8 Hz vs their 1–10 Hz** (`d_equiv.py:5-8`); **linear stacking only, no phase-weighted stacking** (`k_grow.py:50-136`); **3 iterations vs their 10–15** (`k_grow.py:234`); **no 10 %/5 s dedupe** in the running chain; **no ≥100-detection template gate**.

**Status. Queued, not started** (`notes/MARGIN_CATALOG_PLAN_2026-09-24.md` row 6).

**To reproduce from here.** Implement the Savard & Bostock cross-station detector, seed from ALP0, ALS0, BWO0 over 2009-08-03→09-17, add phase-weighted stacking and the 10–15-iteration convergence rule. Reference lines: 18 families, >15,000 detections, 13 locatable, 4 free depths at 31–34 km, and the 5 km/day southward front.

## 7. Plourde et al. 2015 — southern Cascadia (40–41.8°N) — *the seed set behind Ducellier*

**What they found.** "A total of **59 initial templates** from Cascadia produced waveforms sufficiently clear to be picked. These 59 templates were found to represent **34 distinct LFE families**. These families comprised **9900 LFEs**, or a mean of **290 per family**, with **870** as the largest number of detections in a single family" (§3). Time span: **21 March – 30 April 2008**. "Cascadia LFEs extend from **40°N to 41.8°N**" and "Hypocentral depths range from **28 km to 47 km**, a larger distribution than in northern Cascadia" (§3). Three further families sit far to the south on strike-slip faults: two on the Maacama Fault at **15 km** and **21 km** depth and one on the northern Bucknell Creek Fault at **16 km**, with 869, 302 and 325 repeats (§4). Falsifiable extras: "The main front of tremor epicenters migrates along strike at **9 km d⁻¹**" and one rapid tremor reversal "at **10–20 km h⁻¹**" (Abstract). **They located them — yes, all 34** (every family is labelled with its depth in Fig. 1), using "a **one-dimensional velocity model modified from Verdonck and Zandt [1994]**" (§2). **The location software is SILENT.**

**Method in one paragraph.** **25 stations from the EarthScope Mendocino Experiment plus 7 permanent Northern California Seismic Network stations** — the individual codes are SILENT (§2). Band **1.5–9 Hz**, sample interval **Δt = 0.025 s** (i.e. 40 sps). Crucially there is **no autocorrelation stage to copy**: candidates came from "a combination of **autodetection methods** [Savard and Bostock, 2015] **and visual identification**", with "windows of **60 s** around these LFEs … used as initial templates" (§2). Templates were then improved by iterated network cross-correlation and stacking, "**phase-weighted stacking … until the final iteration**" and linear after that; per-station taper windows let them work with "a **short window of 12 s**" (§2). **The detection threshold is SILENT — this paper never states a MAD multiple.** Uniqueness used two independent tests: templates are the same family if they share "**>10 % of detections**" (allowing 25 s of lag) **or** if their cross-correlation exceeds **0.3** (§2).

**What they released.** Waveform DOI 10.7914/SN/XQ_2007 only; **catalogue SILENT in the paper** — but Plourde privately shared it with Ducellier, and that shared copy (66 template families) is on our disk inside `catalogs/ducellier_catalog/data/Plourde_2015/templates_list.txt`, with latitudes 39.45–41.72 N, depths 15.0–46.8 km and published uncertainties (`lfe_south/results/SOUTH_TRUTH_INVENTORY.md` §2).

**Are we using their method? NO** — we have no cross-station/visual candidate stage, and this paper supplies no threshold to copy. We do use their **12 s template length** (`k_grow.py:33`, cited to them) and, in the south, their **1.5–9 Hz band** (`lfe_south/scripts/t6_duc_cache.py:25`).

**Status.** Reproduced **indirectly**, through Ducellier — see §8. The 34/66 Plourde families are the objects our southern reproduction recovers.

**To reproduce directly from here.** Would need the Savard & Bostock cross-station detector plus a threshold we would have to choose ourselves (the paper gives none), so a *direct* reproduction is not well posed. Scoring against 59→34 families, 9900 LFEs and 28–47 km depths is.

## 8. Ducellier & Creager 2022 — southern Cascadia, eight years — *our best external validation*

**What they found.** They extended Plourde's April-2008 snapshot to **2004–2011**. They worked from Plourde's **unreduced 66** families, not his published 34: "For each of **66 LFE families** they provided hypocentral locations, lists of stations and channels used to detect LFEs, and the timing of all LFE detections. [Plourde et al.] have later reduced the number of LFE families to 37 …, but we chose to use the initial detections". Six were later dropped ("We eliminate **six families** for which too few permanent stations have good templates"), leaving **60** carried to the end. On disk the released catalogues hold **477,715 detections (2004–2011, permanent networks)** and **107,924 (2007–2009, FAME array)** (`lfe_south/results/SOUTH_TRUTH_INVENTORY.md` §2). **They did not locate anything new** — "We use the same locations as P[l]ourde et al. (2015). We have not tried to relocate the families using the new templates, except for family A" (reply to reviewers). Family A alone was retried and may sit "**30 to 50 kilometers east**" at "**20-25 kilometers**" depth, shallower than the plate boundary, possibly on a crustal fault.

**Method in one paragraph — a pure matched-filter paper, no discovery at all.** Stations: FAME (temporary) plus three permanent networks (BK, NC, PB), one- *and* three-component, channels BHZ/EHZ/HHZ/SHZ, "**less than 100 kilometers away from the epicenter** of an LFE family". Waveforms were detrended, Hann-tapered, **instrument response removed** (the only one of these papers to say so for detection), **band-passed 1.5–9 Hz**, and **resampled to 20 Hz**. Templates: an 80-s cut trimmed to **60 s**, RMS-normalised and linearly stacked; later, for the permanent networks, rebuilt from "the **150** LFE detection times with the best cross-correlation value" and shortened to "**25 to 40 seconds** … average duration equal to **30 seconds**". Detection: per-channel normalised cross-correlation, **averaged over channels**, thresholded at "**eight times the median absolute deviation (MAD) of the stacked cross-correlation**", computed **per one-hour block** ("The threshold is thus different for each hour of data", reply to reviewers). Declustering keeps "only LFEs that are separated by at least **one second**". A second, family-specific filter then requires **cross-correlation × number of live channels** to exceed a per-family bar, calibrated so half the survivors are confirmed by the other catalogue; this removed "about **84 %**" of the FAME detections and "about **63 %**" of the network ones.

**What they released — everything.** Waveform DOIs, and "The Python scripts used to analyze the data and make the figures can be found on the first author's Github account, accessible through Zenodo". That repository is on our disk at `catalogs/ducellier_catalog/`: his detector source, his station tables, his **66 families of templates**, and both detection catalogues. It is the only one of these nine with released code *and* catalogue *and* templates.

**Are we using their method? YES, almost exactly — one parameter differs.**
- band 1.5–9 Hz, 20 Hz, response removed — **matches** (`lfe_south/scripts/t6_duc_cache.py:25,81-82`)
- his own templates, his own station list — **matches** (`t7_duc_mf.py`)
- per-channel normalised cc averaged over channels with a complete gapless record — **matches** (`t7_duc_mf.py:113-125`)
- 8 × MAD — **matches** (`t7_duc_mf.py:26`)
- 1-second declustering, his `clean_LFEs` run-chaining — **matches** (`t7_duc_mf.py:44-52`)
- **MAD block: ours is 12 hours, his is 1 hour** (`t7_duc_mf.py:26`, `BLOCK_H = 12`). His paper says "we download **one hour** of seismic data" and his code loops hour by hour (`catalogs/ducellier_catalog/src/find_all_LFEs_parallel.py:220,227` and the MAD at line 321). The 12-hour figure in his script is only how he chunked the job, not the statistic's window. A 12-hour MAD sets a different bar than an hourly one, and our stricter "gapless for the whole block" channel rule drops a channel for 12 hours where his loses only the affected hour.

## Where the margin-wide catalogue actually stands — honestly

Two of the nine catalogues are genuinely reproduced: **Bostock's southern Vancouver Island set** (58 of 96 open families and 22 of 30 sealed hold-outs, rediscovered blind) and **Ducellier's southern Cascadia set** (mean recall 0.836, 63 of 66 families past the reference line, using his own templates and stations). Those two anchor opposite ends of the margin and they are real results. Everything between them is weaker than the project ledger has been implying. The Olympic result is a *coverage* statistic against a 69-row coordinate file whose provenance our own filename disclaims — not 61 of Chestler's 43 families identified — and an earlier comparison on the same data was negative. Washington is a **failed run, not a null result**: this audit shows it was executed with Bostock's 6-second Vancouver Island window and a 52-station probe set instead of Royer & Bostock's 15-second window and their seven named stations, three of which we have never even downloaded, so it says nothing yet about whether their 122 NW and 54 SW families are recoverable. Central Oregon has not been started, and the one paper covering it reports that the method we would bring **fails there by design**. Mendocino has an existence signal on all 27 families but a detector that is currently producing 12.8 million false alarms against 61,441 real events. The single most consequential finding of this audit is that these nine catalogues are **not one recipe** — window lengths run 6 s to 60 s, thresholds run 8× to 45×MAD on three different statistics, and two of the regions were built with a cross-station detector we have not implemented at all — and that our chain has been applying one region's parameters to all of them.
