## Purpose

From the full 19,648-row Phase 1.1 inventory, identify the subset of stations **actively recording data as of yesterday (2026-04-14)**. PNWstore only extends through 2023, so we query the authoritative real-time source: **EarthScope FDSN** (formerly IRIS DMC).

---

## Why EarthScope, not PNWstore

| Source | Latest data | Suitable for "currently active"? |
|--------|-------------|----------------------------------|
| PNWstore SQLite | 2023-12-31 | No — 15 months out of date |
| EarthScope FDSN | near-real-time | **Yes** — authoritative current status |

PNWstore is a regional mirror that lags behind the data center. For "is this station running today?" we must ask EarthScope.

---

## Method

### FDSN query

```python
client = Client("EARTHSCOPE")
yesterday = UTCDateTime("2026-04-14")
inv = client.get_stations(
    network=",".join(pnw_networks),
    starttime=yesterday - 86400,
    endtime=yesterday,
    level="channel",
    minlatitude=40, maxlatitude=52,
    minlongitude=-130, maxlongitude=-116,
)
```

A channel epoch is "active yesterday" iff:
- `start_date ≤ yesterday`, and
- `end_date is None` (ongoing) **or** `end_date ≥ yesterday`

### Joining with Phase 1.1

We left-join the live channel set against `pnw_station_inventory.csv` on (network, station, location, channel_band). This:
- Preserves every currently-active combo
- Carries over historical metadata (years of data, 3C availability, dv/v score) when present
- Flags combos with **no PNWstore history** via `in_pnwstore=False`

# Step 02b — PNWstore Data Availability & Gap Analysis

**Date:** 2026-04-15
**Notebook:** `notebooks/02b_data_availability.ipynb`
**Phase:** 1.2b (per [[PROJECT_PLAN]])
**Output:** `data/inventory/availability_matrix.parquet`, `data/inventory/availability_rollup.csv`, `data/inventory/shortlist_phase13.csv`

---

## Purpose

Phase 1.2a produced a list of **994 prime candidates** — stations that are (a) currently recording per EarthScope FDSN and (b) historically scored excellent/good by the Phase 1.1 dv/v-capability heuristic. The 1.1 heuristic only knew whether a (station, year) *contained* 3C data from a 4-DOY sample; it did not know whether that year's data was *continuous* enough to use.

Phase 1.2b answers that: for every prime candidate, scan every day of every year in PNWstore, compute per-channel uptime and gap statistics, and derive a strict 3-component co-availability measure. Prune to a shortlist that has demonstrably clean data usable by Phase 1.3.

---

## Data source & query strategy

| Aspect | Choice |
|--------|--------|
| Backing store | `/data/wsd01/PNWstore_sqlite/{year}.sqlite` (44 files, 1980–2023) |
| Table | `tsindex` — one row per mseed file, with `timespans` giving exact covered intervals |
| Index used | `tsindex_nslcse_idx(network, station, location, channel, starttime, endtime)` |
| Query per candidate-year | `SELECT channel, starttime, endtime, samplerate, timespans FROM tsindex WHERE network=? AND station=? AND location=? AND channel LIKE 'BH%'` |
| Why direct SQLite | `WaveformClient.query_waveforms` requires `(year, doy)` → ~3.6M queries. Direct indexed queries: ~20k queries at <1 ms each. |

### Timespans field (key detail)

The `timespans` column stores intra-record coverage as `[unix_start:unix_end],[unix_start:unix_end],...`. Parsing this captures mid-file gaps that `endtime − starttime` would mask. For accurate uptime:

1. Parse `timespans` for every record matching the candidate.
2. Merge overlapping intervals across records.
3. Clip to `[year_start, year_end)`.
4. Sum covered seconds → per-channel uptime fraction.
5. Gaps between merged intervals → gap distribution; count gaps > 1 h, record max gap.

### 3-component co-availability

For each (candidate, year), we compute a daily coverage mask per component (E/N/Z or 1/2/Z) and take `np.minimum.reduce(masks).mean()` → fraction of the year with **all three components present simultaneously**. This is the metric the Phase 1.3 pilot cares about.

---

## Parallelization

One process per year (44 SQLite files). Each worker:
- Opens its `{year}.sqlite` read-only.
- Loops over only the candidates whose `[first_year, last_year]` overlap its year.
- Returns per-channel aggregates + clipped intervals for 3C co-availability.

Clean process isolation (each worker touches exactly one DB file). 26 years of prime-candidate activity → **6,504 station-year jobs** completed in **2m47s** wall clock, bounded by DB-file I/O not CPU. Cascadia's 48 cores are not saturated here — work is fast enough to remain I/O-bound.

---

## Filters → shortlist

A station-band combo **passes** Phase 1.2b iff:
- `n_years_3c_90 ≥ 1` — at least one year where ≥90 % of the year has all three components recording simultaneously
- `samplerate ≥ 20 Hz` — required for the 0.1–2 Hz ambient-noise band

No gap-count cutoff at this stage — we retain gap statistics for Phase 1.3 to weight by.

### Takeaway

PB and OO drop to 0 for "yesterday" purely because their archive ingest runs 1–2 days behind real-time — the stations are operating; the data just hasn't been cataloged at EarthScope yet. That's why we carry the more permissive `latest_ge_2d_ago` flag: **744 combos (~93%)** have clear evidence of current operation.

The 43 combos with no FDSN extent are primarily CN stations whose data is held at the Canadian data center rather than mirrored to EarthScope, plus a handful of UW/UO/CC/PB/C8 edge cases. These are still plausibly live (their channel metadata says so) but we can't confirm waveform delivery from the IRIS/EarthScope endpoint.

For Phase 1.3 pilot selection, recommend using `latest_ge_2d_ago` as the "actively delivering" gate to avoid filtering out PB/OO/IU.COR-class stations with legitimate archive lag.

---

## Limitations

1. **`uptime_3c_coavail` is day-binned**, not second-accurate. Fine for the 90 % threshold; the error is at most one day in either direction.
2. **Gap analysis uses merged intervals per channel**; mismatched gaps *between* components are not separately flagged (captured only implicitly by `uptime_3c_coavail`).
3. **No data-quality check yet** — a station can have 100 % uptime but be dominated by anthropogenic noise, clipping, timing errors, or instrument malfunction. That's Phase 1.3's job.
4. **`first_year`/`last_year` come from Phase 1.1's 4-DOY sample**, so a station active <3 months entirely between sample days would be missed at the year level. Not a concern for dv/v candidates (need ≥1 full year anyway).
5. **2023 mount missing** — SQLite index is queryable, so availability numbers are accurate, but actual waveforms for 2023 cannot be read until `/auto/pnwstore1-wd09` is restored. Phase 1.3 should use a pre-2023 window.

## Post-shortlist pruning: offshore, geography, forward roster

After the 801-combo shortlist was produced, three additional filters were applied to reach the final Phase 1.3 pilot pool.

### Offshore flag (`flag_offshore_v2.py`)

OBS stations produce different noise fields than land stations and belong in a separate pipeline. Initial attempt used Natural Earth 10m land polygons, but those missed small PNW islands (San Juans, Vashon) — UW.LOPEZ, UW.OLGA, UW.MNWA were misflagged as offshore.

**Fix:** `is_offshore = elevation_live < 0`. OBS stations sit on the seafloor with strongly negative StationXML elevation (hundreds of meters below sea level); all PNW land stations — including island stations — have elevation ≥ 0. No PNW land region is below sea level in the 40–52°N / -130 to -116°W extent.

Result: 36 offshore (19 OO + 17 NV) / 765 onshore.

### State classification (`filter_geo.py`)

Used Natural Earth `admin_1_states_provinces` at 10m resolution. For each station `(lon, lat)`, point-in-polygon test against Washington, Oregon, British Columbia, plus neighbors (CA, ID, MT, NV, UT, WY, AB, YT) for sanity labeling.

Shapely 2 STRtree returns **indices** (not geometries), so the classifier iterates `for idx in tree.query(pt): if geoms[idx].contains(pt): ...`.
