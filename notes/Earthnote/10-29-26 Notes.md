
Everything feeds one 4-D product, and every piece does one of three jobs.

**4-D = a 3-D velocity model (where things are) + how it changes over time (when).**

**Job 1: measure change over time.** These are the 4-D data themselves.

|Data|What change it sees|
|---|---|
|LFE timing per slow-slip episode|crust between the LFEs and the stations|
|LFE coda stretching|deeper crust and the slow layer|
|Autocorrelation echoes|the column under each station|
|Receiver-function waveform change|slow-layer contrast under each station|
|Earthquake repeat pairs across years|long-term crustal change|
|**Noise dv/v (the pilot, new)**|continuous, including between slow-slip episodes|

**Job 2: get the 3-D paths right.** This is the static model: updates 5 and 6, Delph, noise tomography.

- A time change is measured along a wave's path. To say which cell changed, we need to know the path, and the path comes from the static model.
- A wrong static model puts a real change in the wrong cell.
- The static model is also the "3-D" half of 4-D.

**Job 3: put the layers in the right place.** This is Bloch and our receiver-function inversions.

- The slow-layer cells must sit where the slow layer actually is.
- Misplaced by 3 km, a real slow-layer change gets diluted, or blamed on the crust above.

**The 4-D run itself (runs 4, 5, 6, final)** combines all of job 1 into one inversion, using the paths from job 2 and the layers from job 3. It produces a velocity-change map per depth and per episode or year, with uncertainties.

**The rest:**

- **The solver test** is only speed. It makes the finer 12-km final run possible.
- **Gosselin** is one science question the 4-D product answers: does the slow layer change after slow slip?

So:

- **noise pilot** = a new job-1 data type;
- **Bloch** = job 3;
- **updates 5 and 6** = job 2;
- **run 5** = where they all come together.
