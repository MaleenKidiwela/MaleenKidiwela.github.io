## How these catalogs were built

Most of these catalogs used waveform correlation and stacking to detect LFEs, then measured P/S arrivals on the clearer stacked waveforms to locate each LFE family. A family represents repeating events from approximately the same source.

### The usual sequence

1. Find initial events through visual inspection, similarity between stations, or array beamforming.
2. Find repeats by correlating those waveforms against continuous recordings.
3. Stack repeats to improve signal quality, then pick or correlate identifiable P and S arrivals.
4. Locate the family using those arrival times and a velocity model.
5. Extend the catalog with template matching, assigning subsequent detections to the family.

### Bostock / Royer — Vancouver Island and Washington

**Detection and arrival measurements.** Network waveform correlation and iterative stacking; P/S arrivals measured on high-quality family templates. Bostock's 2015 catalog used network matched filtering.

**Location method.** The underlying template studies used HYPOINVERSE/Hyp2000 and hypoDD double-difference relocation.

[Methods](https://courses.washington.edu/ess502/RoyerBostock2013EPSL.pdf) · [2015 catalog](https://amtseismo.github.io/pubs/bostock_jgr_2015.pdf)

### Chestler & Creager — Olympic Peninsula

**Detection and arrival measurements.** Array beamforming found initial candidates; repeated correlation and stacking built templates. They measured interstation P/S differential times and station S–P times through waveform correlation.

**Location method.** Grid search in a 3-D velocity model, followed by double-difference relocation between families.

[Methods](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2016JB013717)

### Thomas & Bostock — central Oregon

**Detection and arrival measurements.** Cross-station correlation found initial candidates; iterative network template matching and phase-weighted stacking improved them enough to measure arrivals.

**Location method.** HYPOINVERSE, testing two velocity models. Five of 18 families could not be located; most located families required an assumed plate-interface depth because P arrivals were weak.

[Methods](https://pages.uoregon.edu/amthomas/pubs/thomas_tectonophysics_2015.pdf)

### Ducellier & Creager — southern Cascadia

**Detection and arrival measurements.** Multistation matched filtering using stacked templates. They transferred templates from the temporary FAME array to permanent stations using overlapping recordings.

**Location method.** Their catalog extension used the existing Plourde family locations, rather than independently locating every new detection.

[Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.14581v1)

### Shelly et al. 2025 — Mendocino

**Detection and arrival measurements.** Manually selected initial windows during tremor; iterative template matching and stacking; manual P/S picks on the stacks.

**Location method.** Initial grid search with a layered velocity model, then hypoDD, jointly relocating LFE families and nearby ordinary earthquakes.

[Methods](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025GL116116)

## What this changes for our task

This changes how we should frame our task: a reliable automatic P/S picker on every individual LFE is **not** a prerequisite for a family-based catalog. We can detect repeats, build clear family stacks, and establish family locations from those stacks. We must then distinguish a family's representative location from an independently measured location for each event.
