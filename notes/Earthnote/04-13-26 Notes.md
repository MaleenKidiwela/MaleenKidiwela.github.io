
### Paros planning symposium Day 01

Talks ranged from a lot of different projects 

* borehole sensors and projects to put a borehole in forks and because it's part of the accretionary complex (Harold Tobin)
* Monitoring severe storms and tornadoes using infrasound measurements, radar
	* discussions on potential for seismic signatures observed for tornadoes that may have made contact with the ground
	* discussion of UAVs 
* sea and glacial ice changes from ocean and borehole sensors 
* Borehole gravity measurements/monitoring for co2 injection/sequestration 
* Strainmeter, GPS, InSAR observations to understand when and where levee enhacements and sediment diversion is required to stop flood hazards, also to understand subsidence (Austin Chadwick)
* New zealand - 41 pressure guages, 20 seismometers, 14 CPIES. A-0-A drift removal, use GONDOR data (for slow slip event detection) to study how well this works in POBS instruments (Spar webb)
* National infrasound network,Infrasounds detection of water discharges, north korea uninformed water discharges from a dam monitored using infrasound, nocturnal downward flows, flash floods, airplane turbulence monitoring (Christopher Davis)
* Hazardous weather warning systems, local response is differs significantly between regions, decision -relevant warning systems, improving high resolution forecasts (brenda philips)
* Predicting short term precipitation forecasting, centralized, federalized,wind is the biggest noise source for infrasound measurements, making short term precipitation estimation forecasts (Mike zink)
* Point arrays (t shaped arrays pointing towards offshore, forks array is an example) along the coastline to assess offshore point sources, tornado touchdown tilt-meter arrays (Paul bodin)
* EOs (earth observations) are low spatial resolutions, networks of ground sensors currently work in isolation, does the preconditioning of soil hydromechanics impact the severity of geohazards, can we forecast climate change cascading hazards (Marine denolle)
	* Things we can monitor using multi sensor systems
	* Landslides (Akash), avalanches, flash floods LF <2 Hz, 1-5 Hz
	* sinking coasts, rising sea and extreme flood hazards, liquefaction
* Digital Twins: Urban sea systems, first is  a digital twin of ocean - computational rendering of oceans, faithful representations of the real world systems, constant flow of real time data from the natural systems to the computer, graphical portrayal of what if questions, second part is  digital twin of urban sea system: weather module, socioeconomic module ecological (fishing), water quality, hydrodynamics, hazards: man made and natural, ultimate digital twin checks how each of these subsystems interact, realtime dataflow is very important for understand how digital twin evolves (John Delaney)
