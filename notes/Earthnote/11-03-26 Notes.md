
**What I did**

- Cut all 12,861 August 2024 episode detections of the 98 PGC-site families at PGC, B009, B010 and B011 (three components each), aligned on each family's S arrival. For comparison I also cut the same number of windows at random times.
- For each family, I split its events into two separate groups and stacked each group. I then measured how well the two stacks match at each time after S, for stack sizes from 1 to 128 events and in five frequency bands.
- Where the data stacks match no better than the random-time stacks, the coda has ended.

**What I found**

1. **S alignment is mostly right.** S energy peaks about 0.5 s after the marked S. About 1 in 7 families peaks more than 1 s late, so the coda start should be set per family.
    
2. **LFE coda is coherent only below about 5 Hz.**
    
    - 5–15 Hz has none.
    - 4–8 Hz is weak.
    - 1–5 Hz is best.
    - So the earthquake band pair (1–5 and 5–15 Hz) can't be used.
3. **Single LFEs are too weak for her method.** Two single events match at only about 0.18, against her 0.7 cut. Every "event" has to be a stack.
    
4. **Usable coda length grows with stack size.** At 1–5 Hz, CC stays at 0.3 or above until:
    
    - about 2.5 s after S with 16 events per stack;
    - about 5 s with 64 events;
    - about 7 s with 128 events.
    
    The coda stays faintly above noise until about 12 s. A longer window therefore needs bigger stacks, which costs time resolution.
    
5. **My test by stack duration was flawed.** Most time bins held about one event, so it needs redoing with a minimum number of events per bin.
